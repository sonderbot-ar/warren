> **What you are approving.** Fifteen questions a finance student would ask Warren, in Spanish, and what Warren answers to each. Questions come in four kinds: a reported figure (*dato*), a model estimate (*estimación*), a changed assumption (*cambio de supuesto*) and an event (*evento*). Warren shows how it read the question before computing anything. Changed assumptions are recomputed by tested code. Events are answered only with similar past cases, how many there are, and what to watch: never a number an AI made up. Warren never predicts prices or gives advice. This brief says which questions Warren must answer for a finance student, how each kind is answered, and what the engine still needs. ## 1. Who asks, and in what language

- **The student.** An undergraduate or master's student in finance or business at a Latin American university, in or after a first corporate finance course: knows the three statements, DCF and WACC, and has built them in a spreadsheet.
- **The professor.** Sets a company and a date for the class, and wants to show where every number came from and what really happened afterwards. The owner wants both student and professor served (oct.30.2026).
- **Language.** Spanish first, with an English toggle (owner, oct.30.2026); the informal *tú*, chosen as a neutral form (Argentina and Uruguay use *vos*, Colombia often *usted*; a regional setting can change it later). Terms as Latin American courses use them: *ingresos* (or *ventas*), *margen operativo*, *flujo de caja libre*, *costo promedio ponderado de capital (WACC; CPPC en algunos cursos)*, *tasa libre de riesgo*, *prima de riesgo de mercado*, *valor intrínseco por acción*, *puntos básicos (pb)*, *fecha de corte* (as-of date), *rango p10–p90* glossed as "8 de cada 10 resultados deberían caer aquí". Money in *millones de US$*. Number format and spreadsheet formulas follow a **region** setting, apart from the language: Mexico and Central America write 1,234.5 and `=SUMA(A1,B1)`; most of South America writes 1.234,5 and `=SUMA(A1;B1)`. Filing names (10-K, 10-Q, XBRL tag) stay in English with a one-line gloss. Portuguese later (Decision 11). All reader-facing text lives apart from logic (Decision 11, guardrail 5).
- **Companies.** The Phase 1 universe only: Apple, Microsoft, Walmart, Coca-Cola, Procter & Gamble, Johnson & Johnson, Caterpillar, ExxonMobil (`domains/fundamentals/universe.csv`). US GAAP, US dollars. Exxon's filings in Warren stop in August 2026 because the company changed its legal entity (#126); every Exxon answer says so. Latin American companies under IFRS are Phase 2 (Decision 11).

## 2. How Warren sorts a question (backlog #130; goes to the owner)

Every question is read first, and the reading is shown to the student **before anything is computed**. The student can correct it.

| Kind (shown to the student) | #130 term | What it names | How Warren answers | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dato** | literal | A line item or ratio for a company and a reported period | The number as filed, with its filing, tag, period and filing date; flagged when a later filing changed it (#128) | "¿Cuánto vendió Apple el último trimestre?" |
| **Estimación** | literal | A model output for a company on a date: a forecast, a value, WACC, or the model's past forecasts against what happened | The engine's run as of that date, every assumption with its source, and the uncertainty the engine measures for that output | "¿Cuánto vale Microsoft por acción?" |
| **Cambio de supuesto** | literal | A model input and a new value, directly or through a listed transmission | Tested code recomputes with the input changed and shows before, after and the ripple | "¿Y si la Fed sube 25 pb por sorpresa?" |
| **Evento** | hypothetical | An event, past or future, that is not a model input and has no listed transmission | Similar past cases chosen by a rule, what followed against what Warren expected then, how many cases there are, how close each is, and the metrics to watch. **Never a number an AI made up** | "¿Y si los demócratas ganan las elecciones intermedias?" |

**The rule**, checked in this order. A question is a *cambio de supuesto* when every change it asks for is one of the model's inputs with a value, or reaches one through a transmission on the **list of transmissions**. A question that names an event, past or future, is an *evento*. A question with no change and no event is a *dato* when it names a filed line item or ratio, and an *estimación* when it names a model output (a forecast, a value, WACC, or the model's past forecasts).

- **Model inputs** (`domains/analysis/dcf.py`, `DRIVERS` and `SINGLE_VALUES`): revenue growth, operating margin, tax rate, depreciation and amortisation, capex, working capital (each as a share of revenue, by period); risk-free rate, beta, equity risk premium, cost of debt, the tax rate on interest, terminal growth, debt (with what it is for: in v0.9.27 a buyback, so the shares, the market value of equity and beta follow, and the cost of debt is floored at the risk-free rate), cash, minority interest, shares. Two teaching overrides are inputs too: WACC set directly (it then bypasses its parts, and the reading says so) and the terminal value method (Warren's, or Gordon's for contrast). Never inputs: the market value of equity (it comes from the share price, and Warren never edits a price; a debt change moves it only through the shares bought back at that unchanged price) and the roll-forward years (bookkeeping).
- **List of transmissions** (checked into the engine; adding one is an engine change with review and an owner card, because it is a new method under Decision 4). Proposed first and only entry: *an unexpected change in the Fed's policy rate → the 10-year Treasury yield*, Warren's risk-free rate, with a sourced range from the monetary-policy literature (Kuttner 2001; Gürkaynak, Sack & Swanson 2005). An expected move is already in the yield on the as-of date, so it changes nothing; the reading says so.
- So "¿y si el dólar se fortalece?" and "¿y si sube la inflación?" are *eventos* today: neither the exchange rate nor inflation is an input, and no transmission is listed for them.

**Ambiguous questions** are sorted *evento*, and the reading offers the *cambios de supuesto* the student may mean ("¿quieres bajar el margen operativo o el crecimiento de ingresos?"). Warren never quietly turns an event into an input.

**Who sorts.** Decided by the owner on nov.03.2026: the AI reader, built from the start, with menus only as a fallback when it is unsure or unavailable ("no menus first"; Decision 9). The two ways:

1. **An AI reader** (chosen). An AI model reads the question and returns only structured fields: the kind, the company, the date, and the inputs it names. A value is filled in only when it is copied word for word from the student's question, and code checks that it appears there; otherwise the field stays blank for the student to fill in. Code checks the rest against the input list and the transmission list; the student sees the reading as an editable card, confirms or fixes it, and only then does code compute. The AI's output is a label, never a financial number (standing rule). It is a new AI step, accepted by the owner and recorded in Decision 9 (nov.03.2026), and it is measured on a labelled set of student questions (these fifteen and more) before students use it.
2. **Menus only.** The student picks the kind, then the company, date and input from menus, and types the value. No AI, works with today's engine, and the reading card is the same. The cost: a student cannot simply type a question.

Either way the reading card is the same screen. F2 builds the reader with the first screens; the menus are its fallback, never the main way in.

**Point in time.** Every query behind an answer uses only what was public on the *fecha de corte*. The default is the last day that has ended in New York (the engine refuses a day still in progress). Asking about a past date is an explicit choice, and then what happened afterwards is shown beside the estimate (#128).

**Which uncertainty each answer shows.** Revenue and operating income forecasts: the p10–p90 range from Warren's own past misses (M9), with that company's measured coverage. Value per share: the values at the equity risk premium ± one standard error, labelled "sensibilidad a la prima de riesgo, no un rango de resultados", and, when news shocks are applied, the simulation's p10–p90. WACC, cost of equity and beta: point values, labelled as estimates with their method, and the cost of equity also at the risk premium ± one standard error. A reported figure: none, but a flag when it was revised. An event: the number of cases and the thin-evidence flag.

## 3. The questions

Fifteen questions, written as a student would ask them, with an English gloss. "Hoy" (today) says whether the engine answers it now: **Sí** (an engine command answers it; the app only needs to call it), **En parte** (the data exists but not the query or the output), or **No**. ### Datos: what the filings say

**1. ¿Cuánto vendió Apple en su último trimestre y de qué reporte sale ese número?**
*How much did Apple sell last quarter, and which filing does that number come from?*
- Kind: Dato.
- Returns: revenue for the last reported quarter, its 10-Q or 10-K (accession), the XBRL tag, the period end and the filing date; the first-reported and the latest figure, flagged when they differ (the engine knows *that* it changed, not *why*). Apple's fiscal fourth quarter has no 10-Q: it is the year in the 10-K minus the first nine months, and is marked as derived.
- Hoy: **Sí** (since v0.9.6): `python -m domains.analysis reported --ticker AAPL --period latest-quarter --as-of <fecha> --json`, or the call `domains.analysis.reported.reported_period`. Returns the filing (form, accession, date, EDGAR link), first-reported and latest with `changed`, and the derived fiscal Q4 with its inputs. Request R1 (#140), done. Since v0.9.45 (#290) a derived quarter's `value` is the figure a run reads, worked out on one basis, so the reported view and a run agree (J&J FY2022Q4 revenue as of 2023-11-01: 23,706 million in both); the screen shows `value` as the number and puts `plain` (the newest figures as filed, 34,892 million) with `basis_note` in the side panel, saying "presentación original" or "presentación nueva", never "reexpresado". Since v0.9.48 (#282) a line can carry `sign_notes`: a version filed with its sign flipped was skipped, so the figure comes from an older filing than the newest (today only Walmart's income tax, years ending Jan 2009 to Jan 2011 and their fourth quarters, and Caterpillar's Q2 2009 cost of revenue); the Spanish and the "valor etiquetado" label are in `docs/designs/backlog-282-sign-flip-note.md`, Product impact. Since v0.9.49 (#297) a line can carry `meaning_note` (`tag_changed_meaning`): the tag behind the figure changed what it measures, so a change from the first reported is a different line, not a restatement (today only Exxon's revenue: FY2017, FY2018 and some 2017–2019 quarters). Since v0.9.55 (#324) a line can carry `tag_note` (`quarter_on_year_tag`): a fourth quarter the company reported only under a different tag from its year is worked out on the year's tag, so the four quarters measure the same line (today only Walmart's revenue for FY2010Q4, FY2011Q4 and FY2012Q4; the latest view moved, e.g. FY2010Q4 from 112,826 to 113,622 million, while its first-reported 113,651 was already a derivation and stays). Notes on a line or ratio follow the order below Q2.

**2. ¿Cómo ha cambiado el margen operativo de Coca-Cola en los últimos cinco años frente al de Procter & Gamble?**
*How has Coca-Cola's operating margin changed over five years compared with P&G's?*
- Kind: Dato (two companies, a ratio).
- Returns: operating margin by fiscal year for both, as reported (one-off charges included, and said so), each year traced to its filing, the formula, and the fiscal-year ends side by side (Coca-Cola: December; P&G: June, so the "same" year is six months apart).
- Hoy: **Sí** (since v0.9.6): the `reported` command once per fiscal year and company (`operating_margin`, with its formula, the lines it uses and their filings; fiscal-year labels are each company's own, and since v0.9.51 and v0.9.52 match the company's own names in early years too: Walmart's year ending January 2014 is FY2014, Apple's year ending September 2008 is FY2008, Caterpillar's 2007 is FY2007, and no label names two years). The app asks five periods per company, and builds the spreadsheet formula a student would type from `uses` (design principle 5); it never computes the ratio. Request R1 (#140), done. Net margin can sit beside it since v0.9.19 (`net_margin`, on net income to the parent, with the `includes_discontinued_operations` note; #157); a database ingested before v0.9.19 shows it as the coded gap until it is re-ingested. Since v0.9.50 (#297 item 2) the app can ask the five years in one call per company, `domains.analysis.reported.reported_series` (or `python -m domains.analysis series --ticker KO --period FY2021 ... --json`): each period is exactly a `reported_period` answer, plus series notes. Its note `series_mixes_meanings` says when the periods do not all measure revenue the same way (today only Exxon: between FY2016 and FY2017 in years, between FY2018Q2 and FY2018Q3 in quarters; the recent five years of every company carry none). Since v0.9.54 (#297 item 13) a second series note, `series_mixes_vintages`, says when a later filing recast revenue for some years of the series and not others (Microsoft, J&J, Exxon, Coca-Cola and P&G on older windows; none on the last five years). Both go above the chart, see the series paragraph below.

**Notes on a reported figure (Q1 and Q2), in this order.** A figure can carry several notes at once (Exxon FY2018Q4 revenue growth carries three). The side panel shows them most serious first, because the first one is the one a student reads:

1. **Can't be read as asked.** `tag_changed_meaning` on revenue growth (v0.9.46): the two periods measure different lines, so the growth is not comparable. Suggested Spanish: "los dos periodos miden líneas distintas: no son comparables"; when the note's `mixed` is not empty: "{periodo} se calcula con cifras de antes y después del cambio: no son comparables", naming the period from `mixed`. In the same group, a line's `meaning_note` (v0.9.49) whose `meaning` is `mixed`: "Este trimestre mezcla dos definiciones de la línea" (no real case today). They come first because they say the number should not be read as asked at all.
2. **Why it comes from where it does.** In this order:
   - a line's `meaning_note` with `first_reported_differs` true (v0.9.49): the first-reported figure measured another line. With `whole_change` true, suggested Spanish: "Exxon cambió lo que mide la etiqueta XBRL us-gaap:Revenues en el 10-Q del 6 nov 2019: la primera cifra publicada (US$ 279,332 millones) medía otra línea, que Exxon sigue presentando con otra etiqueta (ver el 10-Q). No es una corrección de cifras: son dos líneas distintas." The panel links the 10-Q (the note's `accession_no`) and glosses "etiqueta XBRL" once ("el código con que la empresa marca cada cifra en su reporte"), as a tooltip. With `whole_change` false or null, hedge: "al menos parte de la diferencia se debe al cambio de etiqueta, no a una corrección de cifras". With `plain_meaning` `mixed`, add: "Este cálculo mezcla cifras de antes y después del cambio." Company, number and date are built from the params and the region's format, never a fixed string;
   - `tag_note` (`quarter_on_year_tag`) on the line, `uses_quarter_on_year_tag` on a ratio (v0.9.55): the company gave this fourth quarter only under another tag, so Warren works it out as the year less the first nine months, all on the year's tag. Suggested Spanish, adapted from the engine's (`docs/designs/backlog-324-quarter-tags.md`, Product impact) after the student reviewer asked which figure to use and why two figures for the same revenue are not an error: "Ingresos del cuarto trimestre del ejercicio {año} (trimestre que termina el {period_end}): {value}. Warren los calcula como el ingreso del año menos los primeros nueve meses, con la misma etiqueta XBRL ({tag}) que el año y los otros trimestres, para que los cuatro trimestres midan lo mismo. El {form} presentado el {filed_at} da este trimestre como {reported_value}, pero con otra etiqueta ({reported_tag}). Warren no revisó qué incluye cada etiqueta, así que las dos cifras pueden medir conceptos algo distintos. Si comparas o sumas trimestres, usa {value}; si citas el {form}, la cifra que aparece allí es {reported_value}." "Cuarto trimestre" in words, not "4.º". "Etiqueta XBRL" carries the same tooltip as in the `meaning_note` above, since this may be the first note a student sees. The panel links the {form} (`accession_no`) and shows both figures. It never says the other tag is narrower or broader: the engine ranks tags, it does not check what they include (professor, #324). Unlike a `meaning_note`, it does not replace the "cambió" sentence: the tag note explains the gap to the filing's own quarter, not a change from the first reported (Walmart FY2010Q4 carries both: first reported 113,651, now 113,622 after the year was recast by 0.01%), so a line with `changed` keeps that sentence in group 3. Two updates (nov.12.2026, v0.9.58, #329): (a) the quarter's name and how it is worked out come from the note's `period`, never a fixed "cuarto": Q4 is "el ingreso del año menos los primeros nueve meses" (or "menos los tres primeros trimestres" when the engine derived it that way, `facts.py` Q4 = FY − (Q1+Q2+Q3)), Q3 "los primeros nueve meses menos el primer semestre", Q2 "el primer semestre menos el primer trimestre" ("segundo", "tercer", "cuarto trimestre" in words), since a Q2 or Q3 could carry the note (Walmart's three fourth quarters are the only ones today); (b) when `reported_value`, `form`, `filed_at` and `accession_no` are null (Warren knows the other tag but did not find the filing's figure; never on real data today), the panel drops the sentences citing the filing, shows no filing link and one figure only: "… para que los cuatro trimestres midan lo mismo. En su reporte, la empresa da este trimestre con otra etiqueta ({reported_tag}), pero Warren no encontró esa cifra, así que no la muestra. Warren no revisó qué incluye cada etiqueta. Si comparas o sumas trimestres, usa {value}." "Etiqueta XBRL" keeps its tooltip here too (student reviewer). A quarter whose own tag disagrees with its year by more than 0.5% stays as filed with no note (none today; a note for it is #336, which the panel would show in this group);
   - `sign_flip_skipped` on the line, `uses_sign_checked_line` on a ratio (v0.9.48): a version with the opposite sign was skipped, so the figure is not the newest filing's;
   - `basis_note` on the line, `uses_one_basis_line` on a ratio (v0.9.45): the quarter was worked out on one basis, and the plain figure differs.
3. **It moved.** `changed` on the line, `uses_changed_line` on a ratio (v0.9.6): the figure changed from first reported. Last, because the middle group often explains it. When a line also carries a `meaning_note` with `first_reported_differs`, that note replaces the "cambió" sentence (Exxon FY2018 revenue: 279,332 first reported, 290,212 now). Otherwise the panel says "cambió respecto de la primera cifra publicada", never "reexpresado": a change is not always a restatement.

Notes the engine adds later go into this list by the same test: does it say the number cannot be read as asked (first), why it comes from where it does (middle), or that it moved (last). These notes avoid "reexpresión" altogether: students read it as any recast of past figures, so even a denial can suggest an error. They say "corrección de cifras" in the negative instead.

**Notes on a series (Q2, and Q1 when a few quarters are shown together).** A series note is about the whole chart or table, so it goes above it, before any single figure's notes, and is read before them. `series_mixes_meanings` (v0.9.50) is in the first group: across the break, revenue and the margins on it cannot be compared. Suggested Spanish: "En esta serie, la etiqueta XBRL de ingresos de {empresa} no mide lo mismo en todos los periodos: {old} usan la definición anterior y {new} la definición nueva, desde que el {form} presentado el {fecha} cambió lo que mide la etiqueta. Los ingresos y los márgenes calculados sobre ellos no son comparables de un lado al otro del cambio." For `mixed_periods`: "{mixed} mezcla las dos definiciones." For `other_tag_periods`: "{other} usa otra etiqueta; aún no sabemos qué incluye." The note always names the periods and the filing date in the same sentence. The chart marks the break, and the mixed and other-tag periods, with a mark or a pattern, never colour alone (design principles, accessibility). Lines are not named ("ventas", "total de ingresos") until the engine reads labels from the filings (#297 item 5).

`series_mixes_vintages` (v0.9.54, #297 item 13) comes right after any `series_mixes_meanings`, one per recast in the order the engine gives (`recast_on`), and is in the same first group: a later filing changed revenue figures already published for some years of the series but not for the others, so the two sides may not be on the same footing (Microsoft FY2016–FY2017 after ASC 606, J&J after Kenvue, Exxon, Coca-Cola and P&G on older windows; no company's recent five years). Suggested Spanish, adapted from the engine's (`docs/designs/backlog-297-series-vintages.md`, Product impact) into short sentences after the student reviewer got lost in one long one and asked what to do: "En esta serie no todos los ingresos siguen los mismos criterios. With `mixed_periods`: "{mixed} combina cifras de antes y después del cambio." Then, always: "No calcules crecimiento ni compares márgenes de un lado al otro del cambio sin revisar el {form}: puede que no midan lo mismo. Warren no sabe por qué cambiaron las cifras (por ejemplo, una escisión, una desinversión, una norma nueva de reconocimiento de ingresos o una corrección); el {form} lo explica." {redujo} or {aumentó} follows the sign of `largest_move`; {años} on a year series, {trimestres} on a quarter series (v0.9.56, #325). On a quarter series `largest_move` is a quarter's move whenever the filing moved a quarter the company published, and then the sentence reads "(hasta 3,1 % de la cifra anterior)" with no clause; when `largest_move_on` is `year` the filing moved only full years, so the clause ", medido en el año completo" and the sentence "Ninguna cifra trimestral publicada cambió." are added (split in two after the student reviewer found the clause read like a sentence inside brackets), so a student does not look for a quarter that moved by that much (the engine's suggestion, "ningún trimestre etiquetado", is reworded because a student does not know what a tagged quarter is). A filing that moved only half years or nine-month figures gives no note on a quarter series (J&J FY2022–FY2023 and P&G FY2011–FY2012 lose a false note); the share in the region's format (8,2 %). It says "puede que no midan lo mismo", not "no son comparables", because unlike a tag change Warren does not know what the recast changed. "Mismos criterios", not "misma base" (reads as an accounting basis) or "presentación" (kept for a one-basis quarter, and kept out of series notes since v0.9.50). The {form} links to the filing. The chart marks the line between `before_periods` and `after_periods`, and each of `mixed_periods`, with a mark or pattern, not colour alone. Each year's `changed` flag stays last in its own panel; the series note now explains it, so a series screen no longer has to lean on that flag alone.

### Estimaciones: what the model says

**3. ¿Cuánto vale Microsoft por acción según un DCF, y qué supuestos usa?**
*What is Microsoft worth per share on a DCF, and what assumptions does it use?*
- Kind: Estimación.
- Returns: value per share with its values at the equity risk premium ± one standard error; the five-year drivers; WACC; the terminal value method; every assumption with its source. The market price appears only as an input to the capital weights, never as a target.
- Hoy: **Sí**: `python -m domains.analysis dcf --ticker MSFT` (no AI, free). Without `--as-of` it uses the last day that has ended in New York and says so, with the date of the last EDGAR download (since v0.9.7, #128). A past date is asked with `--as-of <fecha>`; the full run (`python main.py run`) then also shows the forecast against what was reported afterwards. Since v0.9.53 (#297 item 1) a run can carry the note `run_mixes_meanings` (on `run_notes(conn, run_id)`): the revenue it is built on does not measure the same thing in every period, so its `parts` (`seed`, `operating_margin`, `history`) say which assumptions sit across the break. Today only Exxon runs dated 2019-11-15 to 2021-08-01, so Microsoft's run, as Q3 asks it, carries none. It is shown as an estimate note, above the value, before any figure's notes (see "Notes on an estimate" below Q6).

**4. ¿Cuál es el WACC de Walmart y de dónde sale cada parte?**
*What is Walmart's WACC, and where does each part come from?*
- Kind: Estimación.
- Returns: risk-free rate (10-year Treasury, FRED DGS10, its date), beta (method and window), equity risk premium (realised S&P 500 over 10-year bonds since 1989, with its standard error), cost of debt, the weights and the formula. It says that debt is weighted at book value, where textbooks prefer market value.
- Hoy: **Sí** (the run report's "Cost of capital" section).

**5. ¿Cuánto venderá Apple en los próximos cuatro trimestres, y qué tan seguro es ese pronóstico?**
*How much will Apple sell in the next four quarters, and how sure is that forecast?*
- Kind: Estimación.
- Returns: the baseline revenue path, quarter by quarter, with its p10–p90 range from Warren's own past misses known on that date (M9), and how often such ranges held: across all companies 83% (a p10–p90 range should hold about 8 times in 10), and Apple's own figure beside it, since coverage differs by company (4 of the 8 companies fell outside 65–95%, the band the team set as acceptable for an 80% range; v0.9). So the screen says plainly when Apple's own ranges held less or more often than the pooled 83%.

## 4. Out of scope, and how Warren says so

- **Prices and "is it cheap?".** "¿Va a subir la acción de Apple?", "¿Está cara o barata?" Warren never predicts prices or gives advice (owner, oct.14.2026). It answers: "Warren no pronostica precios ni dice si comprar. Te muestro su valor intrínseco, su rango y de qué supuestos depende."
- **Other companies.** "¿Cuánto vale Mercado Libre?" Not in the universe yet. It lists the eight companies and says Latin American companies under IFRS are planned.
- **Today's news as a number.** "¿Cuánto le afecta la noticia de hoy?" Answered as an *evento* (past cases), never as an AI estimate.
- **Concepts** ("¿qué es la deuda neta?") are answered in the side panel's *Concepto* tab of the number they belong to (design principles), not as questions of their own.
