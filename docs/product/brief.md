## 1. Who asks, and in what language

- **The student.** An undergraduate or master's student in finance or business at a Latin American university, in or after a first corporate finance course: knows the three statements, DCF and WACC, and has built them in a spreadsheet.
- **The professor.** Sets a company and a date for the class, and wants to show where every number came from and what really happened afterwards. The owner wants both student and professor served (oct.30.2026).
- **Language.** Spanish first, with an English toggle (owner, oct.30.2026); the informal *tú*, chosen as a neutral form (Argentina and Uruguay use *vos*, Colombia often *usted*; a regional setting can change it later). Terms as Latin American courses use them: *ingresos* (or *ventas*), *margen operativo*, *flujo de caja libre*, *costo promedio ponderado de capital (WACC; CPPC en algunos cursos)*, *tasa libre de riesgo*, *prima de riesgo de mercado*, *valor intrínseco por acción*, *puntos básicos (pb)*, *fecha de corte* (as-of date), *rango p10–p90* glossed as "8 de cada 10 resultados deberían caer aquí". Money in *millones de US$*. Number format and spreadsheet formulas follow a **region** setting, apart from the language: Mexico and Central America write 1,234.5 and `=SUMA(A1,B1)`; most of South America writes 1.234,5 and `=SUMA(A1;B1)`. Filing names (10-K, 10-Q, XBRL tag) stay in English with a one-line gloss. Portuguese later (Decision 11). All reader-facing text lives apart from logic (Decision 11, guardrail 5).
- **Companies.** The Phase 1 universe only: Apple, Microsoft, Walmart, Coca-Cola, Procter & Gamble, Johnson & Johnson, Caterpillar, ExxonMobil (`domains/fundamentals/universe.csv`). US GAAP, US dollars. Exxon's filings in Warren stop in August 2026 because the company changed its legal entity (#126); every Exxon answer says so. Latin American companies under IFRS are Phase 2 (Decision 11).

## 2. How Warren sorts a question (backlog #130; goes to the owner)

Every question is read first, and the reading is shown to the student **before anything is computed**. The student can correct it.

| Kind (shown to the student) | #130 term | What it names | How Warren answers | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dato** | literal | A line item or ratio for a company and a reported period | The number as filed, with its filing, tag, period and filing date; flagged when a later filing changed it (#128) | "¿Cuánto vendió Apple el último trimestre?" |
| **Estimación** | literal | A model output for a company on a date: a forecast, a value, WACC, or the model's past forecasts against what happened | The engine's run as of that date, every assumption with its source, and the uncertainty the engine measures for that output | "¿Cuánto vale Microsoft por acción?" |
| **Cambio de supuesto** | literal | A model input and a new value, directly or through a listed transmission | Tested code recomputes with the input changed and shows before, after and the ripple | "¿Y si la Fed sube 25 pb por sorpresa?" |
| **Evento** | hypothetical | An event, past or future, that is not a model input and has no listed transmission | Similar past cases chosen by a rule, what followed against what Warren expected then, how many cases there are, how close each is, and the metrics to watch. **Never a number an AI made up** | "¿Y si los demócratas ganan las elecciones intermedias?" |

**The rule**, checked in this order. A question is a *cambio de supuesto* when every change it asks for is one of the model's inputs with a value, or reaches one through a transmission on the **list of transmissions**. A question that names an event, past or future, is an *evento*. A question with no change and no event is a *dato* when it names a filed line item or ratio, and an *estimación* when it names a model output (a forecast, a value, WACC, or the model's past forecasts).

- **Model inputs** (`domains/analysis/dcf.py`, `DRIVERS` and `SINGLE_VALUES`): revenue growth, operating margin, tax rate, depreciation and amortisation, capex, working capital (each as a share of revenue, by period); risk-free rate, beta, equity risk premium, cost of debt, the tax rate on interest, terminal growth, debt (with what it is for: in v0.9.27 a buyback, so the shares, the market value of equity and beta follow, and the cost of debt is floored at the risk-free rate), cash, minority interest, shares. Two teaching overrides are inputs too: WACC set directly (it then bypasses its parts, and the reading says so) and the terminal value method (Warren's, or Gordon's for contrast). Never inputs: the market value of equity (it comes from the share price, and Warren never edits a price; a debt change moves it only through the shares bought back at that unchanged price) and the roll-forward years (bookkeeping).
- **List of transmissions** (checked into the engine; adding one is an engine change with review and an owner card, because it is a new method under Decision 4). Proposed first and only entry: *an unexpected change in the Fed's policy rate → the 10-year Treasury yield*, Warren's risk-free rate, with a sourced range from the monetary-policy literature (Kuttner 2001; Gürkaynak, Sack & Swanson 2005). An expected move is already in the yield on the as-of date, so it changes nothing; the reading says so.
- So "¿y si el dólar se fortalece?" and "¿y si sube la inflación?" are *eventos* today: neither the exchange rate nor inflation is an input, and no transmission is listed for them.

**Ambiguous questions** are sorted *evento*, and the reading offers the *cambios de supuesto* the student may mean ("¿quieres bajar el margen operativo o el crecimiento de ingresos?"). Warren never quietly turns an event into an input.

**Who sorts.** Decided by the owner on nov.03.2026: the AI reader, built from the start, with menus only as a fallback when it is unsure or unavailable ("no menus first"; Decision 9). The two ways:

1. **An AI reader** (chosen). An AI model reads the question and returns only structured fields: the kind, the company, the date, and the inputs it names. A value is filled in only when it is copied word for word from the student's question, and code checks that it appears there; otherwise the field stays blank for the student to fill in. Code checks the rest against the input list and the transmission list; the student sees the reading as an editable card, confirms or fixes it, and only then does code compute. The AI's output is a label, never a financial number (standing rule). It is a new AI step, accepted by the owner and recorded in Decision 9 (nov.03.2026), and it is measured on a labelled set of student questions (these fifteen and more) before students use it.
2. **Menus only.** The student picks the kind, then the company, date and input from menus, and types the value. No AI, works with today's engine, and the reading card is the same. The cost: a student cannot simply type a question.

Either way the reading card is the same screen. F2 builds the reader with the first screens; the menus are its fallback, never the main way in.

**Point in time.** Every query behind an answer uses only what was public on the *fecha de corte*. The default is the last day that has ended in New York (the engine refuses a day still in progress). Asking about a past date is an explicit choice, and then what happened afterwards is shown beside the estimate (#128).

**Which uncertainty each answer shows.** Revenue and operating income forecasts: the p10–p90 range from Warren's own past misses (M9), with that company's measured coverage. Value per share: the values at the equity risk premium ± one standard error, labelled "sensibilidad a la prima de riesgo, no un rango de resultados", and, when news shocks are applied, the simulation's p10–p90. WACC, cost of equity and beta: point values, labelled as estimates with their method, and the cost of equity also at the risk premium ± one standard error. A reported figure: none, but a flag when it was revised. An event: the number of cases and the thin-evidence flag.

### Datos: what the filings say

**1. ¿Cuánto vendió Apple en su último trimestre y de qué reporte sale ese número?**
*How much did Apple sell last quarter, and which filing does that number come from?*
- Kind: Dato.
- Returns: revenue for the last reported quarter, its 10-Q or 10-K (accession), the XBRL tag, the period end and the filing date; the first-reported and the latest figure, flagged when they differ (the engine knows *that* it changed, not *why*). Apple's fiscal fourth quarter has no 10-Q: it is the year in the 10-K minus the first nine months, and is marked as derived.
- Hoy: **Sí** (since v0.9.6): `python -m domains.analysis reported --ticker AAPL --period latest-quarter --as-of <fecha> --json`, or the call `domains.analysis.reported.reported_period`. Returns the filing (form, accession, date, EDGAR link), first-reported and latest with `changed`, and the derived fiscal Q4 with its inputs. Request R1 (#140), done. Since v0.9.45 (#290) a derived quarter's `value` is the figure a run reads, worked out on one basis, so the reported view and a run agree (J&J FY2022Q4 revenue as of 2023-11-01: 23,706 million in both); the screen shows `value` as the number and puts `plain` (the newest figures as filed, 34,892 million) with `basis_note` in the side panel, saying "presentación original" or "presentación nueva", never "reexpresado". Since v0.9.55 (#324) a line can carry `tag_note` (`quarter_on_year_tag`): a fourth quarter the company reported only under a different tag from its year is worked out on the year's tag, so the four quarters measure the same line (today only Walmart's revenue for FY2010Q4, FY2011Q4 and FY2012Q4; the latest view moved, e.g. FY2010Q4 from 112,826 to 113,622 million, while its first-reported 113,651 was already a derivation and stays). **Notes on a series (Q2, and Q1 when a few quarters are shown together).** A series note is about the whole chart or table, so it goes above it, before any single figure's notes, and is read before them. `series_mixes_meanings` (v0.9.50) is in the first group: across the break, revenue and the margins on it cannot be compared. Los ingresos y los márgenes calculados sobre ellos no son comparables de un lado al otro del cambio." For `mixed_periods`: "{mixed} mezcla las dos definiciones." For `other_tag_periods`: "{other} usa otra etiqueta; aún no sabemos qué incluye." The note always names the periods and the filing date in the same sentence. The chart marks the break, and the mixed and other-tag periods, with a mark or a pattern, never colour alone (design principles, accessibility). Lines are not named ("ventas", "total de ingresos") until the engine reads labels from the filings (#297 item 5).

`series_mixes_vintages` (v0.9.54, #297 item 13) comes right after any `series_mixes_meanings`, one per recast in the order the engine gives (`recast_on`), and is in the same first group: a later filing changed revenue figures already published for some years of the series but not for the others, so the two sides may not be on the same footing (Microsoft FY2016–FY2017 after ASC 606, J&J after Kenvue, Exxon, Coca-Cola and P&G on older windows; no company's recent five years). Suggested Spanish, adapted from the engine's (`docs/designs/backlog-297-series-vintages.md`, Product impact) into short sentences after the student reviewer got lost in one long one and asked what to do: "En esta serie no todos los ingresos siguen los mismos criterios. El {form} presentado el {fecha} {redujo|aumentó} ingresos ya publicados de algunos {años|trimestres} (hasta {movimiento} de la cifra anterior{, medido en el año completo}){: {moved}}.{ Ninguna cifra trimestral publicada cambió.} {before}: cifras anteriores a ese cambio; Warren no tiene una versión posterior. {after}: cifras de ese {form} o posteriores." With `mixed_periods`: "{mixed} combina cifras de antes y después del cambio." Then, always: "No calcules crecimiento ni compares márgenes de un lado al otro del cambio sin revisar el {form}: puede que no midan lo mismo. Warren no sabe por qué cambiaron las cifras (por ejemplo, una escisión, una desinversión, una norma nueva de reconocimiento de ingresos o una corrección); el {form} lo explica." {redujo} or {aumentó} follows the sign of `largest_move`; {años} on a year series, {trimestres} on a quarter series (v0.9.56, #325). On a quarter series `largest_move` is a quarter's move whenever the filing moved a quarter the company published, and then the sentence reads "(hasta 3,1 % de la cifra anterior)" with no clause; when `largest_move_on` is `year` the filing moved only full years, so the clause ", medido en el año completo" and the sentence "Ninguna cifra trimestral publicada cambió." are added (split in two after the student reviewer found the clause read like a sentence inside brackets), so a student does not look for a quarter that moved by that much (the engine's suggestion, "ningún trimestre etiquetado", is reworded because a student does not know what a tagged quarter is). A filing that moved only half years or nine-month figures gives no note on a quarter series (J&J FY2022–FY2023 and P&G FY2011–FY2012 lose a false note); the share in the region's format (8,2 %). It says "puede que no midan lo mismo", not "no son comparables", because unlike a tag change Warren does not know what the recast changed. "Mismos criterios", not "misma base" (reads as an accounting basis) or "presentación" (kept for a one-basis quarter, and kept out of series notes since v0.9.50). The {form} links to the filing. The chart marks the line between `before_periods` and `after_periods`, and each of `mixed_periods`, with a mark or pattern, not colour alone. Each year's `changed` flag stays last in its own panel; the series note now explains it, so a series screen no longer has to lean on that flag alone.

### Estimaciones: what the model says

**3. ¿Cuánto vale Microsoft por acción según un DCF, y qué supuestos usa?**
*What is Microsoft worth per share on a DCF, and what assumptions does it use?*
- Kind: Estimación.
- Returns: value per share with its values at the equity risk premium ± one standard error; the five-year drivers; WACC; the terminal value method; every assumption with its source. The market price appears only as an input to the capital weights, never as a target.
- Hoy: **Sí**: `python -m domains.analysis dcf --ticker MSFT` (no AI, free). Without `--as-of` it uses the last day that has ended in New York and says so, with the date of the last EDGAR download (since v0.9.7, #128). A past date is asked with `--as-of <fecha>`; the full run (`python main.py run`) then also shows the forecast against what was reported afterwards. Since v0.9.53 (#297 item 1) a run can carry the note `run_mixes_meanings` (on `run_notes(conn, run_id)`): the revenue it is built on does not measure the same thing in every period, so its `parts` (`seed`, `operating_margin`, `history`) say which assumptions sit across the break. Today only Exxon runs dated 2019-11-15 to 2021-08-01, so Microsoft's run, as Q3 asks it, carries none. It is shown as an estimate note, above the value, before any figure's notes (see "Notes on an estimate" below Q6).

**4. ¿Cuál es el WACC de Walmart y de dónde sale cada parte?**
*What is Walmart's WACC, and where does each part come from?*
- Kind: Estimación.
- Returns: risk-free rate (10-year Treasury, FRED DGS10, its date), beta (method and window), equity risk premium (realised S&P 500 over 10-year bonds since 1989, with its standard error), cost of debt, the weights and the formula. It says that debt is weighted at book value, where textbooks prefer market value.
- Hoy: **Sí** (the run report's "Cost of capital" section).

**5. ¿Cuánto venderá Apple en los próximos cuatro trimestres, y qué tan seguro es ese pronóstico?**
*How much will Apple sell in the next four quarters, and how sure is that forecast?*
- Kind: Estimación.
- Returns: the baseline revenue path, quarter by quarter, with its p10–p90 range from Warren's own past misses known on that date (M9), and how often such ranges held: across all companies 83% (a p10–p90 range should hold about 8 times in 10), and Apple's own figure beside it, since coverage differs by company (4 of the 8 companies fell outside 65–95%, the band the team set as acceptable for an 80% range; v0.9). So the screen says plainly when Apple's own ranges held less or more often than the pooled 83%.
- Hoy: **Sí** for the baseline's range (v0.9.8, #144): any run gets its p10–p90 per quarter, how often such ranges held across all companies and for the company, and a coded verdict against 65–95%. On a pipeline run with shocks, the range on top of each simulation (v0.9.16, #167: `run_ranges(conn, run_id, sim_id=S)` or `ranges run --sim-id S`; show `*_center` as the forecast, label `coverage_is_baseline`), and the pipeline report's section 7. Which simulation goes with a variant: `run_simulations(conn, run_id)` and its `primary` pick per variant, masked first (v0.9.18, #201), so the screen goes from a `run_id` to its simulation with no checkpoint read. Since v0.9.53 the forecast carries `run_mixes_meanings` when its revenue mixes two definitions; `seed` in its `parts` marks the revenue path (see "Notes on an estimate" below Q6). The p10–p90 range itself may still be measured on past actuals of the old definition (#297, forecast errors, open).

**Two runs of the same date (v0.9.64, #357, and v0.9.67, #365; Q3, Q5 and Q8).** Every stored run keeps the fingerprint of the concept map it was made with (`concept_map_sha256` in the run's snapshot), and rebuilding it reads its facts through that map. When it shows two runs of one company and date side by side, it compares them in this order and says so once above them, naming the current one by the day it was calculated (the newest run is not always the current one: two runs on v1 and v2 while today is v3):

- *Both versions recorded and different:* "Estas dos estimaciones son de la misma fecha, pero Warren cambió cómo lee los reportes entre una y otra. La calculada el {fecha} usa la lectura corregida: usa esa. Cada una conserva sus supuestos y, al recalcularla con ellos, da el mismo valor que ya mostraba; los dos valores pueden diferir porque parten de lecturas distintas. La anterior ya no se puede volver a leer de los reportes con las reglas de entonces." The higher version is the corrected one. The old v0.9.64 sentence "Las dos se pueden reproducir tal como se hicieron" is not used here: old code is not kept, so the older run's assumptions recompute to the cent but cannot be read again from the filings.
- *Either version NULL, unless both runs share one clean code version* (the same `code_version`, neither `unknown` nor ending `+dirty`): "Estas dos estimaciones son de la misma fecha. Puede que Warren haya cambiado cómo lee los reportes entre una y otra: no guardó sus reglas de lectura cuando hizo la anterior. Cada una conserva sus supuestos y, al recalcularla con ellos, da el mismo valor que ya mostraba." No "usa esa" here: Warren cannot say which reading is current. Never "cambió" or "corregida" here, since nothing says the reading changed. - *Same reading rules (or one clean code version), different map fingerprints:* the v0.9.64 note stands, with the current run named: "Estas dos estimaciones son de la misma fecha, pero Warren corrigió cómo lee una línea de los reportes entre una y otra. La calculada el {fecha} usa la lectura corregida: usa esa. Las dos se pueden volver a calcular desde los reportes, cada una con sus reglas de entonces." Here that last sentence is true, because old maps are kept. "Corrigió … una línea" against the first case's "cambió cómo lee los reportes", so the two notes do not read alike (student reviewer).
- *Otherwise:* no note. The app never says "same"; it only stays silent.

No note when a student sees one run alone. The run's source line can show "Reglas de lectura: v{n}" (or "no registradas" for NULL) beside the fingerprint's first 12 characters, as the run report does, with the tooltip "versión de las reglas con que Warren leyó los reportes, y su código de verificación" ("huella" is not course vocabulary; student reviewer). "¿Qué cambió? (en inglés)" can open the row of `domains/analysis/reading_rules.md` for that version (a fixed table: fingerprint, version, date, what changed and why), in English until it has a Spanish column. No such pair exists among stored runs today: v0.9.68 is the first version change (v2), and it changed no stored run.

Runs stored before v0.9.53 (the 27 Exxon runs dated 2019-11-15 to 2021-08-01 among them) keep the notes they printed when made, so they do not carry it (#319 asks for a dated list of notes added later); until then a screen showing one of those runs calls `reported_series` with the run's `as_of` over the run's quarters (the `FYyyyyQn` labels of its `history:revenue` assumption rows, 11 or 12 today, at most 20; a series cannot mix years and quarters, and the run's three years alone never carry the break) and, if `series_mixes_meanings` comes back, shows that series note above the estimate instead, worded "Los ingresos que usa esta estimación…" (not "de los que parte", since on most of these runs only the margins sit across the break). **Each estimate note teaches (v0.9.60, #197; Q3, Q5 and Q8, and every screen that shows a run's notes).** Every run note now carries `group` (on `run_notes(conn, run_id).to_dict()`), and `domains.analysis.text.WARNING_LESSONS[code]` gives its lesson: `why` (English, one or two sentences with `{name}` placeholders), `params` (the engine's thresholds: a float is a fraction, 0.1 is 10% and 0.03 is "3 puntos porcentuales"; an int is a count, 135 is "135 días"), `reference` (a published source, or null) and `warren_rule` (where a Warren working rule is written, or null). The split, agreed at the engine's design review: the engine owns the English, the group, the params and the sources; the app writes its own Spanish per code in `webapp/text/es.json` under its own keys and fills every number from `params` in the region's format (design principles, "Numbers"), so no threshold is ever typed by hand. The screen shows the notes under three headings, in the order of `WARNING_GROUPS`, and inside each group in the order `run_notes` gives (the notes' stored positions), with one exception the app makes itself: `run_mixes_meanings` always goes first under "Los datos de partida", as the paragraph above says, although the engine stores it after the other data notes (gatekeeper, `math_engine.py`). The headings: "Los datos de partida" (`data`: the figures the run reads), "Supuestos fuera de lo habitual" (`assumption`) and "Cuánta confianza darle al valor" (`valuation`). A heading with no notes is not shown. Each note reads in two layers: the short note first (what happened, on this run), then "¿Por qué importa?", which opens the lesson (why it matters for the value). Under the lesson, the *Fuente* line shows `reference` when set, and "Criterio propio de Warren (no es una norma publicada)" when `warren_rule` is set (the engine sets at least one of the two on every lesson, so the line is never empty), so a student never reads one of Warren's thresholds (the 135 and 161 days, the 3 points) as a textbook law; the rule's own text cites repo files and is for the owner, not the student. The app tests its Spanish keys against `WARNING_LESSONS`'s keys, as it does against `RUN_NOTE_CODES`, so a new code fails a test until it has Spanish; a reworded English `why` is not caught yet (#344 asks for a revision mark per lesson, an `anchors` field so the side panel repeats a note only on the assumptions it is about, and a `check` field apart from `why`).

- *`shares_not_positive`: a count filed as zero or below.* Title: "Acciones en cero o negativas". With `divisor` true: "Warren dejó fuera una cifra de acciones: las acciones {de la portada | diluidas} del periodo que cerró el {period_end} se presentaron como {value}, y una empresa que cotiza no puede tener cero acciones o menos. Warren divide por otra cifra mayor que cero; el supuesto de acciones dice cuál. [Ver reporte]". With `divisor` false (a diluted count that only fed the check beside a cover count), the last sentence becomes: "Esa cifra solo servía para comparar con la de la portada, así que el valor por acción no cambia." `{de la portada | diluidas}` comes from `count`; `{value}` is shown as filed, in the region's number format; "[Ver reporte]" opens `accession_no`. Lesson (no params): "¿Por qué importa? Ninguna empresa que cotiza puede tener cero acciones o menos, así que esa cifra es un error del reporte. Si Warren dividiera por ella, el valor por acción sería infinito o no tendría sentido. Por eso la deja fuera y divide por otra cifra mayor que cero: el supuesto de acciones (pestaña *Fuente*) dice cuál." *Fuente*: "Criterio propio de Warren (no es una norma publicada)" (no `reference`).
- *No count above zero at all:* since v0.9.68 the run is refused instead of crashing, but only with English text ("no share count above zero reported"). The app never shows the engine's English. Until the refusal has a code (#376), the screen says only "Warren no pudo calcular esta estimación con los reportes disponibles hasta esa fecha. Prueba con otra fecha." The student reviewer asked for the cause ("ninguno de los reportes trae un número de acciones válido"); not taken for now, because without a code the app cannot tell this refusal from the others (no price on the date, before `SUPPORTED_FROM`), and a wrong cause would mislead more than none. With #376 each refusal gets its own sentence.

### Cambios de supuesto: change an input, see the ripple

**7. ¿Qué le pasa al WACC y al valor de Caterpillar si la Fed sube la tasa 25 puntos básicos por sorpresa?**
*What happens to Caterpillar's WACC and value if the Fed unexpectedly raises rates by 25 bps?*
- Kind: Cambio de supuesto, through the listed transmission.
- Reading shown first: "Cambio: tasa libre de riesgo, por una subida inesperada de la Fed. Traslado a la tasa a 10 años: entre A y B pb por cada 25 pb (fuente). Si la subida ya se esperaba, la tasa de hoy ya la incluye y no cambia nada." A and B come from the engine's sourced range (R2c), never from the app.
- Returns: before and after for the risk-free rate, cost of equity, WACC, terminal growth and value per share, at the low and the high end of the transmission range, with the reason for each move. It names the run's terminal value method and what follows from it. Two of Warren's own choices, not general rules, shape the answer, and the screen says so: terminal growth is set equal to the risk-free rate (the usual ceiling, Damodaran), and new investment after year five earns exactly its cost of capital (RONIC = WACC: the return on new invested capital equals its cost, so growth adds no value), which makes terminal value NOPAT × (1 + g) ÷ WACC. So growth moves with the rate but barely matters, and the higher cost of equity, hence WACC, drives the change. The cost of debt does not move: Warren takes it from past interest over average debt.
- Hoy: **En parte** (since v0.9.9, #141): `python -m domains.analysis whatif --run-id R --add risk_free=0.0025 --json` (or `domains.analysis.whatif.revalue`) moves the risk-free rate and shows cost of equity, WACC and value per share before and after, with terminal growth and the growth fade following it and each moved line's cause. The app must label it "if the 10-year yield moved by 25 bp": the Fed's surprise to the 10-year transmission, with its low and high ends, is R2c (#143).

### Eventos: answered from past cases

It always shows the number of cases (n) and a thin-evidence flag when n is small. Cases are chosen by a stated rule (event type and tags), not by hand.

## 4. Out of scope, and how Warren says so

- **Prices and "is it cheap?".** "¿Va a subir la acción de Apple?", "¿Está cara o barata?" Warren never predicts prices or gives advice (owner, oct.14.2026). It answers: "Warren no pronostica precios ni dice si comprar. Te muestro su valor intrínseco, su rango y de qué supuestos depende."
- **Other companies.** "¿Cuánto vale Mercado Libre?" Not in the universe yet. It lists the eight companies and says Latin American companies under IFRS are planned.
- **Today's news as a number.** "¿Cuánto le afecta la noticia de hoy?" Answered as an *evento* (past cases), never as an AI estimate.
- **Concepts** ("¿qué es la deuda neta?") are answered in the side panel's *Concepto* tab of the number they belong to (design principles), not as questions of their own.
