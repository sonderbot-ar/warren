# Backlog #170: a leverage what-if (more or less debt, with beta relevered)

Issue: https://github.com/sonderbot-ar/warren/issues/170 · Branch `backlog-170-leverage` · Version v0.9.27 when merged · Part 1 of 2.

## Problem

Brief §2 (`docs/product/brief.md`) lists debt among the inputs a student may change. The what-if (v0.9.9, `domains/analysis/whatif.py`) refuses it on purpose: one row, `debt`, is both the WACC's debt weight and the claim on the firm, and beta is not relevered. The #141 review measured what a naive change does: on WMT_2026-10-15_1, debt +$50bn moved WACC 9.15% → 8.89% and value per share $30.03 → $24.99. More debt looked cheaper because the equity's risk stayed where it was; and on a run with no debt, whose stored cost of debt is 0 ("not used (weight 0)", `math_engine._cost_of_debt`), the new debt would cost nothing.

A student's question is "if Walmart borrowed $50bn to buy back shares, what is a share worth?" Answering it honestly needs three things the naive change lacks: what the money is for, a beta that rises with leverage, and a price for the new debt.

## Scope: part 1 and part 2

- **Part 1 (this item):** the debt swapped with equity at the as-of share price (a buyback, or new shares that repay debt), beta relevered by Hamada's rule, the cost of debt held at the company's own but never below the risk-free rate unless the student sets it, and refusals where an answer would mislead.
- **Part 2 (a follow-up issue, filed with this PR):** a cost of debt that moves with leverage on its own: a synthetic rating from interest coverage and a dated spread by rating (Damodaran's method, *Applied Corporate Finance*, ch. 8). It needs a dated, sourced spread table for every case date, which is new data behind an adapter and its own design. Until then, part 1 says plainly that the cost of debt was held and what that leaves out.

## What changes

1. **`Change("debt", delta=…)` or `value=…`, with `use="buyback"`.** `Change` gains `use`, required for `debt` and refused on any other input (`use_on_other_input`). The one use in part 1 is `buyback`: the change in debt is swapped with equity at the run's as-of price (the stored `price` row): more debt buys shares back, less debt is repaid with new shares. Other uses are refused with `debt_use_not_supported` (params `use`, `supported`): `cash_kept` (the engine weights gross debt, so debt held as cash would raise beta and the debt weight while net debt and the business are unchanged; it needs net-debt weights, a design of its own) and `acquisition` (it changes the cash flows, which a what-if holds). A debt change without a use is refused with `debt_use_needed`.
2. **The rows the child changes** (ΔD = new debt − the parent's debt; price P = the parent's `price` assumption row, the as-of close that `math_engine` stores beside the valuation rows and does not value with; a run without a positive price is refused with `no_price`). `math_engine` stores `equity_market_value` as exactly P × `shares` (`math_engine.py`, the market rows), so the child computes E′ = P × N′ and the identity E′ = E − ΔD holds to float precision. Shares are a count of value, not certificates: N′ is not rounded.
   - `debt`: D′ = D + ΔD, at least 0 (`out_of_range`, low 0 included).
   - `shares`: N′ = N − ΔD ÷ P (how `buyback`); `equity_market_value`: E′ = P × N′ (how `buyback`), so E′ = E − ΔD at the same price. If E′ would not be above zero the change is refused with `buyback_exceeds_market_value` (params `debt_change`, `equity_market_value`): a buyback can't retire more than every share.
   - `beta`: relevered (how `relevered`), Hamada (1972) with the tax term, as Damodaran teaches it (*Investment Valuation*, ch. 8): unlevered βu = βL ÷ (1 + (1 − t) D ÷ E) on the parent's rows, then βL′ = βu × (1 + (1 − t′) D′ ÷ E′) on the child's, with t the `debt_tax_rate` and E the market value of equity. D is book debt, as the engine's WACC weights it (brief §3, Q6, says so); two new `financial_math` functions, `unlevered_beta` and `relevered_beta`, with property tests.
   - `cost_of_debt`: held at the parent's unless the student sets or adds to it in the same what-if, **but never below the risk-free rate** (analyst, design review; must-fix). The stored cost is past interest over book debt (`math_engine._cost_of_debt`), and on 7 of 8 companies' latest runs it is below the 4.98% risk-free rate (XOM 1.47%, JNJ 2.20%, PG 2.56%, MSFT 3.11%, AAPL 3.37%, KO 3.69%, WMT 4.76%; CAT 5.42% is above): held as is, more debt would cost less than riskless debt, which breaks Modigliani–Miller even with no tax and turned XOM's answer the wrong way (a buyback at $187 of shares Warren values at $116 raised value per share). So when debt changes, the student did not name the cost of debt, and the stored cost is below the child's risk-free rate, the child's `cost_of_debt` is the risk-free rate (how `floored_at_risk_free`, note `cost_of_debt_floored_at_risk_free`). This prices the whole debt, old and new, at the floor, as a lender would reprice it; it is also Hamada's own assumption (riskless debt). A run whose stored debt is 0 has cost of debt 0 as a placeholder, so new debt on it is refused with `cost_of_debt_needed` (params `debt_before`) unless the student sets or adds to `cost_of_debt`.
   - Nothing else: the cash flows, cash, minority interest and the rate inputs are the parent's. Free cash flow to the firm is held, which is the WACC method's convention (the tax saving on interest enters through the after-tax cost of debt, not the cash flows); EV then changes only through WACC (Modigliani and Miller 1958, 1963), and value per share through WACC, net debt and the share count.
   - With ΔD = 0 a child is stored (as for any change of zero) and only the debt row's method text differs from the parent's.
3. **Inputs that follow and so can't be changed beside it.** `beta` and `shares` are computed by the leverage change; naming either beside `debt` is refused with `set_by_debt_change` (params `name`; not `follows_…`, which is a `how` prefix, product lead). `wacc` set directly is refused beside it with the existing `wacc_with_rate_input` (params `name` `debt`), since a set rate ignores the weights. `cost_of_debt`, `debt_tax_rate`, the drivers, cash, minority interest, terminal growth and the terminal-formula switch may be changed beside it.
4. **What-if notes**, on every debt change (refusals are checked in the order listed in item 1–3, then bounds):
   - `leverage_changed` (params `use`, `debt_before`, `debt_after`, `shares_before`, `shares_after`, `price`, `beta_before`, `beta_after`, `unlevered_beta`, `debt_tax_rate_before`, `debt_tax_rate_after`, `cost_of_debt_before`, `cost_of_debt_after`, `debt_weight_before`, `debt_weight_after`, `interest_coverage_before`, `interest_coverage_after`): the swap, the price, the weights (D ÷ (D + E)) and beta relevered, with every term of Hamada's formula (product lead). Interest coverage is year-1 forecast EBIT over cost of debt × debt (None with no interest): no rating is read from it in part 1, but it shows the strain (professor). The text says the beta is the run's Blume-adjusted beta, the tax rate the run's effective rate (not the marginal rate Damodaran prefers; analyst), the debt riskless in Hamada's rule, debt at book as the run counts it (operating leases out), and that of the usual rules Hamada gives the largest tax saving.
   - `buyback_at_market_price` (params `price`, `value_per_share_before`, `value_per_share_at_parent_enterprise_value`, `swap_effect`, `enterprise_value_effect`): shares are bought or sold at the market price, not at Warren's value, so when Warren's value per share is below the price a buyback moves value away from the shareholders who stay, and above it towards them. The change in value per share is split in numbers (product lead, analyst): the swap at the parent's enterprise value (new debt and shares, the parent's cash and minority interest), and the rest (`enterprise_value_effect`: the changed enterprise value from the new WACC and any other input, plus any cash or minority interest changed beside the debt; gatekeeper). On every Phase 1 company Warren's value is below the price, so the swap dominates.
   - `cost_of_debt_floored_at_risk_free` (params `before`, `after`), when the floor applied.
   - `cost_of_debt_held_on_leverage` (params `cost_of_debt`, `floored`; when floored, the text says it rose only to the floor, gatekeeper), when debt rises and the student did not set the cost of debt: lenders charge more as debt rises, and with no cost of financial distress the fall in WACC is the tax saving on interest before those costs, so it overstates the gain (Damodaran, *Applied Corporate Finance*, 4th ed., ch. 8). It replaces the risk-free what-if's `cost_of_debt_held` on a debt change, so one held number has one reason (product lead).
   Units: `debt_*`, `price`, `value_per_share*` and the effects are in the what-if's `currency` (`price` and value per share per share); `shares_*` a count; betas, weights, rates and coverage are plain numbers. `beta`, `debt`, `shares`, `equity_market_value` and `cost_of_debt` before and after are in `changes`, not `lines`.
5. **Sources and trace.** Each changed row gets a `what_if` source with `before` and `how` (`set`/`add` for debt, `buyback`, `relevered`); `sources.for_child` already does this. A copied row derived from a changed one names the parent's row, as today.
6. **`driven_by`.** `debt`, `beta`, `shares` and `equity_market_value` are added to the lines they feed: cost of equity (beta), WACC and every line discounted by it (debt, beta, market value), net debt, equity value and value per share (debt), value per share (shares).

**Point in time.** Nothing is read but the parent's stored rows and outputs, which were point in time on its date; the price is the parent's `price` row.

**LLMs.** None. The student names the debt; `financial_math` does every number.

**Downstream.** The child is an ordinary run: `runs.reproduce`, the sensitivity grid and a Monte Carlo read its rows like any other. No migration.

## Alternatives considered

- **Harris–Pringle or Miles–Ezzell (βL = βu (1 + D ÷ E), no tax term)**, the rules for a company that keeps its debt *ratio* steady (Miles and Ezzell 1980, *JFQA*; Harris and Pringle 1985, *J. Financial Research*; Koller, Goedhart and Wessels, *Valuation*, 7th ed., the appendix on levered and unlevered cost of capital). Strictly, a single constant WACC implies a constant ratio, and Hamada's fixed dollar debt held for ever does not match it; of the three, Hamada gives the largest tax-shield gain (Fernández 2004, *J. Financial Economics*; professor). Hamada with the tax term is kept because it is what Damodaran teaches with the cost-of-capital approach (*Investment Valuation*, 3rd ed., ch. 8), the text most Latin American finance schools use, and what the issue names; the note says it is the upper bound. Switching is one function.
- **A synthetic rating now.** It is the right cost of debt (Damodaran, *Applied Corporate Finance*, 4th ed.: the rating from coverage in ch. 4, the optimal mix in ch. 8), but it needs a dated spread table for every case date (new data, an adapter, sources); it is part 2. The risk-free floor is the least part 1 can do without new data.
- **Refuse a book cost of debt below the risk-free rate** instead of flooring it (analyst's other option). Rejected: it would refuse 7 of 8 companies unless the student names a rate, and the floor is Hamada's own assumption.
- **Debt as a plain input with beta held** (the naive change). Rejected in #141 for the reason above.
- **Market-value weights for debt.** The engine weights debt at book (brief §5); changing that moves every stored WACC and is not this item's question.

## Results on real data (Step 3, nov.06.2026)

On the restored snapshot (schema 21, `warren-20261105T101750Z`), on the latest stored run of each company (as of 2026-10-30), value per share before → after:

- **ΔD = 0 on all eight:** value per share unchanged to the cent (AAPL 95.21, CAT 218.45, JNJ 168.01, KO 37.37, MSFT 203.55, PG 104.13, WMT 31.24, XOM 116.23).
- **Debt +10% of the market value of equity:** AAPL 95.21 → 69.11 (WACC 11.15% → 10.97%), CAT 218.45 → 178.93 (10.79% → 10.61%), JNJ 168.01 → 154.70 (6.61% → 6.67%), KO 37.37 → 31.20 (6.92% → 6.91%), MSFT 203.55 → 172.26 (11.45% → 11.28%), PG 104.13 → 97.54 (7.43% → 7.45%), WMT 31.24 → 22.09 (8.87% → 8.68%), XOM 116.23 → 109.65 (6.50% → 6.44%). The floor applied on every company but CAT (its stored cost of debt is above the risk-free rate). Every result falls, and the swap is most of it (AAPL −27.71 of −26.10; CAT −44.92 of −39.52): Warren values every share below its price, so a buyback at the price moves value away from the shareholders who stay. JNJ and PG's WACC rises: the floor lifts their cheap old debt's cost by more than the tax saving.
- **WMT_2026-10-15_1, +$50bn (the #141 review's case):** beta 0.695 → 0.725 (unlevered 0.664), debt weight 5.8% → 10.8%, interest coverage 12.1 → 5.9, the cost of debt floored 4.76% → 5.19%; WACC 9.15% → 9.06% and value per share $30.03 → $25.53 (swap −4.96, enterprise value +0.47). The naive change in #141 gave 8.89% and $24.99. With the cost of debt set at 6%: 9.12%, $25.21; at 8%: 9.29%, $24.42.
- Every child reproduces; `python -m domains.analysis reproduce --all`: 922 of 922 runs reproduce to the cent (the stored runs and the 19 children made here, on this machine's copy, not saved to the shared snapshot).
