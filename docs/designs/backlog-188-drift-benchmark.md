# Backlog #188: a benchmark with drift, and a Wilson interval beside the range's record

Issue: https://github.com/sonderbot-ar/warren/issues/188 · Branch `backlog-188-drift-benchmark` · Status: built, in review (nov.09.2026).

## Problem

The track record (v0.9.14, `domains/evaluation/track_record.py`, brief Q6) compares Warren with one benchmark, M3's seasonal random walk: the same quarter a year earlier. For a company that grows, that benchmark is wrong by about a year's growth every quarter, so beating it says little. Microsoft's revenue: Warren's median miss 3.9% against the benchmark's 13.8% (`docs/designs/backlog-145-track-record.md`, Results). The professor at the #145 review asked for a fairer second benchmark: the seasonal random walk **with drift**, one of the models Foster (1977, *The Accounting Review* 52(1):1–21) compared for quarterly accounting numbers; Brown & Rozeff (1979) and Bathke & Lorek (1984) show how close these simple random-walk benchmarks come to fitted ARIMA models.

Second, the range's record per company rests on about 20 calendar-quarter clusters, and a percentile bootstrap undercovers with that few (Efron & Tibshirani 1993, ch. 14; Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2008 on few clusters). When every actual was inside (`all_inside`), the bootstrap collapses to 100–100% and says nothing at all.

## Plan

- **Step 0.** This design doc and its review. No build plan change (backlog item).
- **Step 1. The drift benchmark, per row and per summary.**
  - Forecast for target quarter t from the origin run's own stored history (the 12 quarters the run read, `math_engine.HISTORY_QUARTERS`, as known on `made_on`): **Q(t−4) + δ**, where δ is the mean of the seasonal changes Q(s) − Q(s−4) over every quarter s in the run's history whose year-earlier quarter is also there (8 changes with 12 quarters: two years of drift). This is the seasonal random walk with drift, E[Q_t] = Q_{t−4} + δ, one of Foster's (1977) models (not his preferred one, an AR(1) in seasonal differences: see Alternatives), with δ estimated on the history the forecast had, never on later data. Foster estimated δ on a much longer history; Warren's runs store two years of changes. The eight changes telescope: δ = (the last four quarters' total − the total of the four quarters two years earlier) ÷ 8, so only two annual totals matter, and a 2020 or 2022 quarter at either end moves δ a lot (Exxon, Caterpillar). Revenue and EBIT alike, in money. The track record only has horizons 1–4 (M9's), and there Q(t−4) is always at or before the last reported quarter, so the model needs nothing more.
  - **Changes on one basis only** (analyst). A run's history holds each quarter's latest version as known on its date, so a pair can straddle a recast. On the snapshot, J&J's run of 2024-02-01 holds FY2022Q3 at $20.0B (recast to continuing operations after Kenvue) beside FY2021Q3 at $23.3B (as first consolidated), and a derived FY2022Q4 of $34.9B (a year on the old basis less nine months on the new). `mixed_pairs` takes F, the filing that first reported Q(s), and leaves the pair out when the run's Q(s) differs from F's by more than 0.5% (`BASIS_TOLERANCE`: s was recast later, so it no longer sits beside the s−4 that F printed), when F gives Q(s−4) a value that far from the run's (v0.9.7's test, `hindsight._basis_change`), or, for EBIT, the two quarters were built on different EBIT definitions (`harness._alternative` of each history row). Such a change is left out of δ and named in `left_out`; a pair that can't be checked (no filing found, a comparative derived across vintages) is kept, as v0.9.7 keeps such a quarter. With gaps or left-out changes the telescoping above no longer holds and the remaining changes weigh unevenly; that is said in the note. This runs only when the basis is checked (`check_basis`, the default); `basis_checked` says whether it did. The target quarter's own recast (Q(t−4) against the actual) is the row's existing `basis_change` flag, as for M3's benchmark.
  - At least 4 changes (one year) are required, else `no_drift` (params `period`, `n_changes`, `min_changes` 4). Q(t−4) missing gives the same `no_benchmark` as M3's benchmark (no forecast at all); that gap wins when both apply.
  - Each row item gains `drift`: `forecast` (money), `step` (δ in money: the average change from a quarter to the same quarter a year later), `n_changes` (the changes used), `left_out` (the quarters s whose change was left out), `basis_checked`, `miss` and `signed_miss` in the row's `miss_unit` (revenue: a fraction of the actual; EBIT: a fraction of the actual revenue, i.e. margin points; `harness.score`, as Warren's own miss; positive: the benchmark forecast too high), `closer` (`warren`, `benchmark`, `tie`), `missing`. A tiny or negative Q(t−4) is harmless: misses are scaled by the actual (revenue), never by the benchmark.
  - Each summary gains `drift`: `n_rows` (rows with a drift forecast), `median_miss`, `mean_miss`, `skill` (1 − Warren's mean miss ÷ the drift benchmark's mean miss, both over the rows that have a drift forecast; None when that mean is 0 or there are none), `wins`, `losses`, `ties`, `sign_p` (M8's exact two-sided sign test on |Warren's miss| − |the drift benchmark's|), `verdict`, `missing`, under exactly the rules of the existing test: all horizons tested once per target quarter, h1 on rows, no verdict at h ≥ 2, none under 12 quarters. The existing benchmark, its fields and the `headline` are unchanged: M3's benchmark stays the headline, since it is the one every past report used.
  - Note `benchmark_with_drift` on every record, saying what the second benchmark is.
  - Tests (`tests/evaluation/test_track_record.py`, synthetic runs): the forecast equals Q(t−4) + mean of the stored seasonal changes; a run holding only rows up to the origin uses nothing later; 3 changes give `no_drift`, 4 do not; a missing Q(t−4) gives `no_benchmark` on both; the drift benchmark is exact on a steady grower (constant δ) and so beats M3's; a J&J-shaped recast pair, a prior quarter printed otherwise, and an EBIT pair across two definitions are left out (`mixed_pairs`), a 0.2% difference is kept; fiscal-year history rows are not quarters; signed miss and closer; summary counts add up; verdict rules as M3's benchmark's (h ≥ 2 no verdict; per-quarter test); M3's fields are unchanged: every existing track-record test passes without an edit, and the real-data check in Step 3 compares them with the #145 table; the call checks the basis and leaves a mixed change out end to end (a `FactReads` stand-in). Exit: green.
- **Step 2. A Wilson interval beside the cluster bootstrap.**
  - Each range cell gains `wilson_interval`: the 90% Wilson score interval (z = 1.645, the bootstrap's level) for the share, taking **the number of calendar-quarter clusters as the sample size** (`wilson_n`). Rows in one calendar quarter share one shock, so each cluster is taken as one draw, an effective-sample-size approach (Korn & Graubard 1998, *Survey Methodology* 24:193–201; Wilson at small n: Brown, Cai & DasGupta 2001, *Statistical Science* 16(2)). It is approximate, not a bound: one origin's forecast covers four consecutive quarters, so its miss also carries across clusters (`ranges_report.py`, the block bootstrap), and the true effective n can be lower still. The bootstrap's interval is shown as before, and the verdicts and `target_in_interval` stay on it. With every actual inside it gives a real lower bound (with 20 clusters, about 88%), where the bootstrap gave 100–100%. Gaps as the bootstrap's (no rows: None). The verdicts do not change.
  - Tests: Wilson against a hand-computed value; at share 1 the upper end is 1 and the lower is below 1; `wilson_n` equals `n_clusters`; no cell gets one without rows. Exit: green.
- **Step 3. The command and real data.** The text table gains the drift benchmark's median miss and verdict, and each range cell the Wilson interval. On the restored snapshot, as of 2026-11-02 (the #145 results date): every company's drift summary, written below; M3's benchmark figures equal the #145 table; every drift forecast recomputed independently from `assumptions` with SQL equals the call's (with the left-out changes removed); how many changes were left out per company; an Exxon 2021 row shown (the 2020 impairment at the end of the window). Exit: equalities hold.

**Exit criterion:** all tests pass; Step 3's checks hold; `docs/team-sync.md` names the new fields.

## Alternatives considered

- **Drift from the last four changes only.** A one-year drift follows turning points faster but is noisier; Foster's model estimates δ over the history, and the run's stored eight changes are what it had. Not taken.
- **Replace M3's benchmark.** Rejected: every past report and the M8 review score against it, and changing the headline benchmark is a change to how Warren is judged; the drift benchmark sits beside it.
- **Foster's preferred model, an AR(1) in seasonal differences with drift; a seasonal ARIMA (Brown & Rozeff 1979; Griffin 1977); or analysts' consensus.** Stronger, but with 12 quarters a run has only 7 pairs of seasonal changes to fit the AR coefficient, and consensus is paid data. Not taken.
- **A drift in logs (a growth rate).** For a company growing at a steady rate, a drift in money falls behind; ln Q(t−4) + mean log change would not. But logs fail on a zero or negative EBIT, and the money form is the one in the literature. Not taken; a possible third benchmark.
- **Wilson on rows.** Treats every row as independent: narrower than the bootstrap, so it would not show what few clusters cost. Rejected.

## Product impact

Serves brief Q6 ("¿Qué tan bien ha pronosticado Warren a P&G?") and design principle 4 (show the uncertainty measured). Nothing renamed or removed; no migration; nothing stored. Added fields: row `items.<item>.drift` {`forecast`, `step`, `n_changes`, `left_out`, `basis_checked`, `miss`, `signed_miss`, `closer`, `missing`}; summary `drift` {`n_rows`, `median_miss`, `mean_miss`, `skill`, `wins`, `losses`, `ties`, `sign_p`, `verdict`, `missing`}; range cell `wilson_interval` [lo, hi] and `wilson_n`. New codes: `no_drift` (gap), `benchmark_with_drift` (note). Units: `forecast` and `step` are money in the record's `currency`; `miss`, `signed_miss`, `median_miss` and `mean_miss` are in the row's or summary's `miss_unit` (fractions: 0.05 is 5%, or 5 margin points for EBIT); `skill` is a fraction; `wilson_interval` is a pair of shares. The `headline` cell's benchmark is M3's, unchanged. Suggested words: `drift` "el mismo trimestre del año anterior más el cambio medio anual" (a fairer bar for a growing company); `no_drift` "no hay suficientes trimestres previos para calcular el cambio medio anual"; `benchmark_with_drift` "una segunda referencia suma al trimestre del año anterior su cambio medio anual". Show `wilson_interval` beside the bootstrap's; it matters most when `all_inside` is noted, and its plain reason is "contando cada trimestre calendario una sola vez". Brief Q6's "a simple benchmark" becomes two; the front-end team updates the brief's wording.
