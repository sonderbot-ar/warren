# Backlog #497: Walmart's cost of debt counts its finance-lease interest

Issue: https://github.com/sonderbot-ar/warren/issues/497 (from the #481 analyst review) · Branch `backlog-497-wmt-lease-interest` · Version v0.10.29 when merged.

Status: In progress (nov.22.2026).

## Problem

Warren's cost of debt (D8) is a fiscal year's interest over the average of its two year-end debts (`math_engine.cost_of_debt_year`). For Walmart the two are on different bases:

- **Debt includes finance leases.** `math_engine.debt_parts` adds `finance_lease_liabilities` (`FinanceLeaseLiability`) to debt. Walmart files it at every year end from 2020-01-31 (ASC 842, adopted in FY2020): 4,818M then, 6,761M at 2026-01-31.
- **Interest leaves finance-lease interest out.** Walmart's income statement splits "Interest" into "Debt", "Finance lease" and "Interest income". The map reads WMT's interest from `InterestExpenseDebt` (`interest_expense` priority 3; the note already says "interest on debt only (finance-lease interest excluded)"), because Walmart tags neither `InterestExpenseNonoperating` nor `InterestExpense`.
- **Which lease interest figure.** The income statement's "Finance lease" line (481M FY2026, 479M FY2025) carries Walmart's own tag, `wmt:FinanceLeaseAndFinancingObligationInterestExpense`: it also holds interest on financing obligations, which Warren does not count as debt (and company facts drop extension tags). The lease note (Note 6, Leases) discloses "Interest on lease obligations" as `us-gaap:FinanceLeaseInterestExpense`: 306M for FY2020 (first filed in the FY2020 10-K, 2020-03-20, accession 0000104169-20-000011) up to 383M for FY2026 (FY2026 10-K, accession 0000104169-26-000055, R3 and the lease note; analyst). Its liability is exactly the `FinanceLeaseLiability` Warren counts (6,761M = 856M current + 5,905M non-current on the balance sheet), so Warren uses the note's figure. A student who adds the income statement's lines gets 2,318 + 481 = 2,799M; Warren's 2,701M is 2,318 + 383, and the run's method text names the tag.

So from FY2020 on, Walmart's cost of debt divides debt-only interest by debt plus leases, and reads low. On the newest WMT run (2026-10-30, FY2026): interest 2,318M over average debt 48,657M is 4.76%, just below the 4.98% average 10-year yield, so the `below_risk_free` note fires and a debt what-if floors. With the finance-lease line, 2,701M / 48,657M is 5.55%.

Before FY2020 the two bases already agree: Walmart's capital lease obligations (`CapitalLeaseObligations`, 7,412M at 2019-01-31) are not mapped, so they are not in debt, and their interest (`InterestExpenseLesseeAssetsUnderCapitalLease`) is not in interest either. `FinanceLeaseInterestExpense` and `FinanceLeaseLiability` both start with FY2020 in Walmart's annual filings, so adding the first wherever it is reported keeps the two in step. One year straddles: FY2020's average debt opens at 2019-01-31, before leases were on the balance sheet (as Warren reads it), so its denominator is half on the old basis. Adding the full year's lease interest there would overshoot: 2,568M / 52,545M = 4.89%, against 4.57% on a fully lease-inclusive average (opening 50,621M + 7,412M capital leases) and today's 4.30% (analyst). So the lease interest is added only when the average debt counted the finance-lease liability at **both** year ends (or at the closing one when the opening is not reported): FY2020 keeps today's figure. That year is used by the 13 stored WMT runs dated 2020-04-01 to 2021-03-01.

### Who else files the tag (company facts, nov.22.2026)

| Company | Interest tag Warren reads | Files `FinanceLeaseInterestExpense` | Effect of adding it everywhere |
|---|---|---|---|
| WMT | `InterestExpenseDebt` (debt only) | FY2020–FY2026 | **Fixes the mismatch** |
| MSFT | `InterestExpenseNonoperating` / `InterestExpense` | FY2016–FY2026 (2,547M FY2026) | **Double count**: Microsoft's interest expense already includes finance-lease interest (FY2025 10-K, Item 7: "offset in part by higher finance lease interest expense", accession 0000950170-25-100235; analyst) |
| AAPL, KO, PG, JNJ, CAT, XOM | totals | no (each one's company facts, nov.22.2026) | none |

So the line must be added for Walmart only.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave finance leases out of Walmart's cost-of-debt denominator.** Fixes the ratio but makes D8 compute debt two ways (with leases for net debt and the WACC weight, without for the rate), and the rate would then not describe the debt it weighs. Adding the missing interest keeps one debt.
- **Add `FinanceLeaseInterestExpense` to the `interest` chain as a first alternative (`interest_expense + finance_lease_interest`).** Same numbers, but every other company's interest would become alternative 2, a "fallback", changing the method text of every stored run on rebuild.
- **Rank the tag inside `interest_expense`.** The map picks one tag per item, never a sum.
- **A Walmart rule in the engine.** Puts a company in code; the guardrail is companies as data (Decision 11).
- **Add the lease interest whenever the closing debt counts leases.** Overshoots FY2020, whose average is half on the old basis (analyst, Problem).
- **Read the pre-FY2020 capital lease interest and obligations too.** Would also be consistent, but changes every older Walmart run and their debt, for a mismatch that does not exist there today. Not now.
