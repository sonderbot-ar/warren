# M10 design: more test dates, chosen by rule, some after the models' cutoff

Status: In progress (nov.02.2026) · Elephant: agent team · Branch `m10-more-dates`
Implements the second item of the Phase 2 direction (Build Plan §4b, owner decision nov.02.2026) and the post-cutoff test that Build Plan §7 lists against "LLM knows the outcome of historical events".

## Problem

The Phase 1 answer to "does news make Warren's forecasts better?" is **not shown** (`docs/phase1-review.md`, "The answer"): on 26 cases the AI's shocks cut error by 2.1% (revenue) and 1.5% (EBIT, masked), with sign tests at p 0.79 and 0.82. Three things stop a firmer answer:

1. **Too few cases.** Only 14 (revenue) and 18 (EBIT) of 26 masked cases moved at all; the sign test runs on those. A 2–5% gain can't be told from zero on that count.
2. **The cases were picked in hindsight** as notable events (`docs/designs/m3-evaluation-harness.md`, Case library). Notable events are where news should help most, so the 26 cases can flatter the method, and the controls are only 8.
3. **Every case is before the models' training cutoff** (June 2026 for both models, `shared_kernel/config.py:78-81`), so the named variant's small edge over masked could be the model remembering what happened next (`docs/phase1-review.md`, "Named and masked disagree").

M10 adds test dates chosen by a calendar rule fixed before any result is seen, for every company in the universe, including dates whose whole news week is after the cutoff. Each new date runs through the one-command pipeline (M7), is scored as in M8 (same metrics, same headline) and gets M9's range.

What M10 does not do: no new forecasting logic, prompt, scale or shock rule; nothing is tuned on the results (M6 and M8 rule; calibration waits for held-out cases, backlog #73). Merger sizing (#102), commodity baselines and the Exxon successor link (#126) stay where they are.

## The date rule (fixed here, before any result)

A **grid case** is one company of the universe (`domains/fundamentals/universe.csv`, never a hard-coded list) on one **grid date**:

- **Pre-cutoff grid:** every other 15th of February, May, August and November, from 2019-05-15 (the first such date after the engine's first as-of date, `math_engine.SUPPORTED_FROM` = 2019-03-01) to the last one before the earliest model cutoff month begins: May and November in odd years, February and August in even years, so the kept dates alternate seasons and none is left out (15 dates, 2019-05-15 to 2026-02-15). The first draft took all 29 quarterly dates; the owner capped the AI spend at $10 on nov.03.2026 ("about half"), before any result, and this halving is the rule that follows (`grid._kept`). These are mid-quarter for seven of the eight companies, a week or two after most of them file their 10-Q, so the baseline has just seen the last quarter and the next one (h1) is about half done: news in the week can still change it. Walmart is the exception (its quarters end in January, April, July and October, so its h1 has just ended; analyst review). A fixed calendar means where a date falls relative to filings and news is not chosen; every company is on every date, so one GDELT week serves all of them.
- **Post-cutoff grid:** the 1st and the 15th of every month whose 7-day news window starts after the latest model cutoff month has ended: 2026-07-15, 2026-08-01, 2026-08-15, 2026-09-01, 2026-09-15, 2026-10-01, 2026-10-15 (7 dates). Twice as dense as monthly because these dates are the scarce resource; fixed now, not after a look. A date is **post-cutoff** when its window's first New York day (`as_of − 6 days`) is after the end of the latest model cutoff month among `NODE_MODELS` (`grid.is_post_cutoff`). "Jun 2026" is month-only (`shared_kernel/config.py:78-81`), so data through June 30 is assumed. No extra margin: a stated cutoff can be later than what a model reliably knows (Cheng et al. 2024, "Dated Data", COLM), which makes the post-cutoff group safer, not leakier; a cutoff that is in fact later than stated would leak, and the memory test below classifies each quarter by its outcome date, which keeps a two-week error from deciding the result. A date between the two grids (inside or overlapping the cutoff month) is on neither.
- The last grid date is 2026-10-15 because a case needs at least one forecast quarter with a reported actual. Later dates are added by a future run with the same rule (the generator takes an end date), never by hand.

**Exxon, by the coverage rule.** Exxon's filings here stop on 2026-08-03 (universe note). The harness fails a case whose horizon reaches past that (`harness.guard_coverage`: as-of + 92 × (h + 1) + 45 days). As for M9's origins, a grid case gets the longest horizon, up to 4, that the guard accepts, and is left out where not even one quarter fits: Exxon has h3 on 2025-05-15 and h1 on 2025-11-15, and is left out on the 8 dates from 2026-02-15 (1 pre-cutoff, all 7 post-cutoff). The guard is known to be too strict (#126); this grid follows it, and the left-out cases are listed by `grid` and in the report.

**A grid date shared with a hand-picked case** (none in the halved grid; WMT_2022-11-15 was one in the full grid) stays in the grid: dropping it would be a choice made after looking. It shares that case's run and AI answers, which are keyed by company and date; storing the grid never overwrites the hand-picked row in `eval_cases` (`cases.store_cases`).

**168 grid cases** on 22 dates: 119 pre-cutoff, 49 post-cutoff (8 companies × 22 dates = 176, less Exxon's 8).

**Horizon and scorable quarters.** Up to 4 quarters, as M3 and M9. h1 is the first fiscal quarter after the last one reported on the case date (`harness.py:366-371`); only quarters with an actual reported by the batch's `actuals_as_of` are scored. **`actuals_as_of` is fixed at 2026-11-16** (the batch runs on or after it): by then Apple's fiscal Q4 10-K and Caterpillar's Q3 10-Q, filed in early November in past years, are in, but Walmart's October-quarter 10-Q is not (filed in early December: 2023-11-30, 2024-12-06, 2025-12-03; analyst query). A case with no scorable quarter by then is counted as **not yet scorable**, apart from failed and left-out cases, and re-scored by a later batch; it is never silently absent from "all grid cases" (`backtest.pairs` drops cases without `ok` rows, so the report counts them from the case list). Post-cutoff cases score one or two quarters (the September quarter, reported late October, is h2 for the July and August dates).

## Storage

The grid lives in a checked-in file, `domains/evaluation/grid_cases.csv`, with the same columns as `cases.csv`: `event_type` = `grid`, `description` "Grid date: no event chosen", `selected_because` "grid rule: mid-quarter 15th" or "grid rule: post-cutoff 1st/15th", no source URL. It is **written by the generator** (`python -m domains.evaluation grid --write`) and checked by the loader (`grid.load_grid`), which regenerates it from the rule and the universe and fails if the file differs, so a hand edit can't sneak a date in or out. `grid` is a case type only the grid rule admits (`cases.EVENT_TYPES`; a `grid` row needs `selected_because` "grid rule…", and the hand-picked file may hold none). The grid and the hand-picked file are never loaded together, so the hand-picked controls' 120-day clearance (`cases.load_cases`) does not apply to grid dates. Each batch stores the file's SHA-256 as today. The grid rows go into `eval_cases` like any case. No migration: `eval_cases.event_type` has no check constraint (`0007_evaluation.sql`; analyst confirmed).

## Running the cases

`python -m domains.evaluation run-grid --max-usd X [--from D] [--to D] [--ticker T ...] [--dry-run]` (`domains/evaluation/grid_run.py`) runs the M7 pipeline (`graph.start` with both variants and auto review, since every grid date is more than 7 days before the run) for every grid case not yet done, date by date, so each GDELT week is downloaded once (`fetch_news` reads a window only when it isn't fully read) and then serves all eight companies; `python -m domains.news ingest --grid` downloads the weeks on their own, ahead of the AI step. It reuses everything the pipeline reuses (stored filings, prices, events, answers); a failed case is resumed (`graph.resume`), a done one skipped. The seed is `harness.case_seed(case_id)`, so the simulations are the ones the backtest draws. `--dry-run` lists what would run and estimates the cost at the measured M7 date's 8 cents a case. **The cap is in dollars**, the unit the owner approves: before each case, the tokens of every AI call made since the command started (`llm_calls.created_at`) are priced at the published per-token prices M6 recorded (Haiku 5.5 $0.10 / $0.50, Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10 per million input / output; `grid_run.PRICES_USD_PER_MTOK`, and a model without a price stops the run), and the command stops cleanly when the spend plus a $0.50 reserve for one case would pass the cap, leaving the rest for the next run. The pipeline's own per-step token budget still applies; if it is spent, the run stops too.

**Order:** post-cutoff dates first (they are the new evidence), then the pre-cutoff grid from the newest date back, so a budget stop leaves a usable, dated subset rather than a random one.

**Cost.** Free except the AI calls. The GDELT week for one date is 672 files, about 5.5 minutes (M7 Step 6), so 22 weeks ≈ 2 hours of download (measured: about 6 minutes a week once running). AI: the one measured new date (Walmart, 2025-08-21, M7 Step 6) cost about 4 cents for one variant with 15 events; two variants ≈ 8 cents, so the full 279-case grid was ≈ $15–25. This is above the M6 run ($5.81), so it went to the owner first (CLAUDE.md: anything that costs money). **The owner chose a $10 cap on nov.03.2026**, with about half the pre-cutoff dates: 168 cases ≈ $13 at 8 cents, so the cap may stop the run before the oldest pre-cutoff dates; the run order (post-cutoff first, then newest back) decides which, and the report counts the cases not run. Everything before the AI step (filings, prices, news, events, baselines) is free and runs while the card waits.

## Scoring

One backtest batch over the grid (`backtest --cases-file domains/evaluation/grid_cases.csv`), on the same stored run per case that the shocks were sized on (M8, "Which run"), with four of M8's five variants: `baseline`, `naive_srw`, `llm_named`, `llm_masked` (`with_shocks` needs transcribed company guidance, which grid dates don't have). Every M8 metric, unchanged, from `domains/evaluation/backtest.py`. Then M9's ranges on the batch (`ranges test --batch`), pooled, with the build's errors as known on each case's date.

**The headline (fixed now)**, per item, on **all grid cases** (a case without shocks takes the baseline forecast, M8's intention-to-treat reading, so quiet dates count as ties): the `llm_masked` skill score against the baseline (1 − mean error with shocks ÷ mean error without, pooled over quarters), with `llm_named` beside it. The statistic is M8's. **Its inference is corrected for shared dates** (professor review): the eight companies on one date share a news week and its macro surprise, so cases on a date are not independent (Petersen 2009, *RFS* 22:435; Cameron & Miller 2015, *J. Human Resources* 50:317). The test read for "shown" is therefore:

- the exact sign test on the **per-date** mean of d (d = a case's mean error with shocks minus without, over its scored quarters, as M8), on the dates with a non-zero mean (up to 22);
- a **date bootstrap** 90% interval for the mean d: whole dates resampled, 10,000 draws, seed `20261102`, percentile interval; and a moving-block sensitivity over consecutive dates, blocks of 4 (Künsch 1989), because neighbouring dates forecast overlapping quarters.

M8's case-level sign test and case bootstrap are shown beside, for comparison with v0.8 (on M8's own 26 cases, all on different dates, the two coincide). A gain is **shown** when the date-level sign test has p < 0.05 and the date bootstrap interval excludes zero; otherwise **not shown**, never "no effect".

**What the test can see** (minimum detectable effect, stated now). If about half the cases move and the shocks win M8's share of them (8 of 14, 0.57), the case-level sign test on ~80 moved cases (168 grid cases) has about 25% power at α = 0.05, and less once cases on a date are allowed to correlate (ρ ≈ 0.3 gives a design effect ≈ 3). A win rate of about 0.70 or better on moved cases is what this batch can reliably show; M8's 0.57 is not. Halving the grid for the budget cost power, and the report says so (professor review, I2).

**The memory test** (secondary, fixed now). Memory leaks the **outcome**, not the news (Sarkar & Vafa 2024, "Lookahead Bias in Pretrained Language Models"), so quarters are grouped by when their actual became public, on **h1 only** so that the horizon mix can't move the result (analyst review: post-cutoff cases score mostly h1, pre-cutoff ones h1–h4, and errors grow with horizon). For each case, at h1, g = e(named) − e(masked), where e is the error with shocks minus without at h1 (below 0: named helped more). Groups:

- **A:** news and outcome before the cutoff (the h1 actual's `filed_at` on or before 2026-06-30);
- **B:** news before, outcome after (a pre-cutoff case whose h1 was filed after June; empty at h1 in the halved grid, whose last pre-cutoff date is 2026-02-15, kept so a later grid can fill it);
- **C:** news and outcome after (every post-cutoff case).

Reported: mean g per group with a date bootstrap 90% interval, and C − A. If the named edge comes from memory, g should be below zero in A and near zero in B and C. Masking does not fully stop recall (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu 2025, "The Memorization Problem", arXiv 2504.14765: the model can infer the company from its numbers), so g is a lower bound on memory; the masked headline in A against C is shown as a second signal, confounded by the 2026 regime. C has 7 dates, so few clusters, where cluster bootstraps are unreliable (Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2008, *REStat* 90:414): "no change shown" is reported as "not shown", never as "no memory". The distraction effect of the name (Glasserman & Lin 2023) is present in every group.

**Also reported:** the headline by pre- and post-cutoff; h1 split into quarters that had already ended on the case date and quarters still running (as M9; post-cutoff h1 is often a closed quarter); how many grid cases had no events in the week, events but no material impact, or at least one shock, by date (the share of quiet dates is itself a result; the holiday weeks are thin); M9 range coverage on grid cases (baseline and combined), beside the rolling test; cases left out, failed and not yet scorable, with reasons; scored-quarter counts per group. The cutoff rule (`grid.is_post_cutoff`) replaces M8's looser count in the backtest report (`backtest_report.py:322`, case date ≥ first of the cutoff month); every M8 case is from 2025 or earlier, so M8's report text is unchanged (a test).

**Why the headline statistic does not change from M8's.** Holding the metric, the population rule and the variant fixed is what makes the grid a test of M8's finding rather than a new search. The inference is corrected for the grid's shared dates; on M8's own cases it gives M8's answer.

## Alternatives considered

- **Event dates from SEC 8-K filings** (every 8-K with items 1.01, 2.01, 2.05, 7.01 or 8.01, from the EDGAR submissions file). Hindsight-free and closer to "news days", but each date needs its own GDELT week (no sharing across companies: about 8× the download) and the filing item mix shifts with each company's habits. A good second grid later; the calendar grid is the cleaner first test.
- **Quarter starts** (the 1st of January, April, July, October; the first draft). Rejected in review (analyst): for seven of the eight companies h1 has already ended on that date, so the week's news can't change it and a shock starting in quarter 1 lifts a closed quarter (the start-rule problem M8 sent to the backlog, #115).
- **Earnings-week dates** (the 1st of February, May, August, November, when most of the eight report). News-rich, but the week's headlines report the quarter the baseline has not filed yet (#115 again). The mid-quarter 15th sits just after most of the eight have filed.
- **All 728 M9 origins.** The baselines exist, but 91 news weeks and about $60 of AI calls, for overlapping monthly dates whose errors are strongly correlated; quarterly dates give most of the independent information for about 40% of the cost.
- **Random dates.** No better than a fixed calendar at avoiding hindsight, and they break the one-week-for-all-companies sharing.
- **Pool the grid with the 26 cases.** Rejected for the headline: the hand-picked cases would weight it towards notable events; they are shown beside.
- **Wait for more post-cutoff quarters.** The post-cutoff set grows every month; the generator's end date moves forward in a later run, under the same rule.
