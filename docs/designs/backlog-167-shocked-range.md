# Backlog #167: the shocked range on pipeline runs, and the range in the pipeline report

Issue: https://github.com/sonderbot-ar/warren/issues/167 · Branch `backlog-167-shocked-range` · Version v0.9.16 when merged.

## Problem

Brief question 5 and design principle 4: the student sees the range of the forecast actually shown. Since v0.9.8 any run has the baseline's range (`run_ranges.run_ranges`, `basis: "baseline"`, note `baseline_only`), but a pipeline run shows a shocked forecast (the Monte Carlo paths, report section 2), and its range is not built. M9 builds the shocked ("combined") range only for backtest cases (`ranges.shocked_samples`, M9 design "With shocks"). And the pipeline report, the one document a run leaves behind, has no range at all: #144 deferred it because `reproduce` compares the report again, and a range moves if the stored misses change.

## What changes

1. **The shocked range on any simulation.** `run_ranges(conn, run_id, n=DEFAULT_SAMPLES, check_basis=True, sim_id=None, coverage=True)`. With `sim_id`, the simulation must be stored and belong to `run_id` (else `EvaluationError`, as for an unknown run). The samples are the simulation's own: `montecarlo.sample_lines(load_assumptions(conn, run_id), load_simulation_shocks(conn, sim_id), n_samples, seed)` with the simulation's stored seed and sample count; **the simulation's `n_samples` replaces `n`** for both the samples and the range draws (M9 uses one n for both). On the restored data the samples' median equals the stored `simulation_outputs.p50` exactly for revenue and EBIT in quarters 1–4 on all 77 pipeline simulations (analyst, nov.05.2026). Each shocked sample gets one drawn past miss, M9's method unchanged (`ranges.ranges` on those samples, the misses filed by the run's date, both pools). `basis` becomes `"shocked"`, `sim_id` names the simulation, and the range seed is `ranges.range_seed(f"{sim_id}:shocked", pool)` (M9's `<subject>:<variant>` key with the simulation as subject). `check_basis` works as for a baseline call. Without `sim_id` nothing changes: `basis` `"baseline"`, `sim_id` None, the v0.9.8 seeds and numbers (a test).
2. **The centre, and the change read from it.** Each quarter keeps `revenue_forecast` and `ebit_forecast` (the baseline's, as before) and gains `revenue_center` and `ebit_center`: the median of the samples the misses are applied to, for every forecast quarter. For a baseline range they are the forecast; for a shocked range, the simulation's p50, the number the report's section 2 shows. **`revenue_vs_forecast` and `ebit_vs_forecast` keep their v0.9.8 meaning** (from the baseline forecast; EBIT over the baseline's revenue forecast). Each band gains `revenue_vs_center` (the band is centre × (1 + x)) and `ebit_vs_center` ((band − EBIT centre) ÷ revenue centre, margin points), equal to the `_vs_forecast` pair on a baseline range (product-lead and professor: a changed meaning under the same name would mislabel the app).
3. **What the shocked range assumes, said plainly.** On a shocked range the note `shocked_plus_misses` (params `sim_id`) replaces `baseline_only`: "the range adds the baseline's past misses to each shocked sample of the simulation; the shocks mostly move where the band sits. When a shock captures an event the baseline would have missed, part of that miss is counted twice, which can widen the band a little." The first draft said "leans wide"; the evidence says otherwise. On 63 pipeline simulations with a pooled quarter-4 band, the median width ((p90 − p10) ÷ centre) is 37.4% for the baseline band and 37.6% for the shocked one, the largest gap 43.3% against 37.8% (MSFT_2023-05-15); the shocks move the centre by up to 10% of revenue (analyst). On M9's 26 cases the masked combined range held 75.5% (revenue) and 84.9% (EBIT), less than the baseline range's 79.2% and 87.0% (M9 results; professor). `coverage_is_baseline`: "the track record is the baseline range's: past ranges were tested without shocks."
4. **`coverage=False`** skips the track record, the slow part (`sample_lines` takes under 0.01 s per simulation; the track record about 5 seconds on the restored data, #144 Step 3). `coverage` is then an empty list and `coverage_skipped` says "the track record was not computed for this call (it is the baseline range's either way)".
5. **Misses the table may not hold yet** (analyst blocker). The misses are scored by M9's origin rebuild, which runs by hand; nothing in a pipeline refreshes it (the newest stored filing is 2026-10-28, `ranges.LAST_ORIGIN` 2026-10-01). A run dated after the newest filing in the table may be missing misses that were already public. Such a run gets `errors_stale` (params `as_of`, `errors_filed_to`, the newest filing in the whole table): "the stored misses end with actuals filed on …; any filed after that and by … are not in the range yet (they come with the next rebuild of the misses)". Every pipeline stored today is dated 2026-10-15 or earlier, so none has it.
6. **The range in the pipeline report.** `PipelineState` gains `range_errors_sha256: str | None`: `ranges.pairs_sha256` of the misses filed by the as-of date, over every company (M9's hash: a rebuilt miss of another company changes it too). The `write_report` node sets it beside `actuals_on`. When it is set, `report.render` calls `run_ranges` on the baseline run (with the pipeline's `n_samples`, `check_basis=False`, coverage on) and once per distinct simulation (`coverage=False`); a simulation shared by named and masked (same shocks, `nodes.simulate` reuses it) is shown once, labelled with both. The section, **"7. Range from past misses"** (the hindsight section is always there for new pipelines, `graph.write_report`; the trace becomes 8), has:
   - one sentence: what the range is, that it stops at quarter 4 while section 2 runs to year 5, and that it is in millions of the run's currency;
   - per basis a table: Quarter | Revenue forecast (the centre) | p10 | p50 | p90 | EBIT forecast (the centre) | p10 | p50 | p90, from the pooled band (M9's headline), with a quarter without a band showing its code. The forecast column and the range's p50 differ by the pooled bias of past misses, and the sentence says so (professor);
   - the track record once, under the baseline table only: the pooled range's share inside for all companies and this company, per item, with interval and verdict; the shocked tables carry the `coverage_is_baseline` text instead (professor);
   - the notes' English text, each once.
   Pipelines finished before this have no `range_errors_sha256`, so their reports render exactly as before (a test), as #128 did with `actuals_on`.
7. **Reproduce names the cause.** `python main.py reproduce --pipeline-id P` adds a line: `OK range misses <first 12 hex>` when the misses known on the as-of date still hash to the stored value; otherwise `MISMATCH range misses: stored …, now …`, counted as a failure (M9's `reproduce_build` rule, "the errors the build read have changed"), with the reason: the misses were rebuilt or rescored after the report was written, and `python main.py report --pipeline-id P` writes it again with the misses as they are now, storing their new hash in the pipeline's checkpoint (`graph.refresh_range`), so the report and the hash it was rendered from stay together and `reproduce` passes again (gatekeeper: the first build rewrote the report but kept the old hash, so the printed remedy never cleared the failure). For a run that had `errors_stale`, a rebuild is the expected cause.

**Point in time.** Unchanged from v0.9.8: misses filed by the run's date, coverage from origins dated before it with actuals filed by it. The simulation's shocks were sized from news known by the as-of date (M6, `known_by`), and the simulation is stored. No look-ahead found in review (analyst).

**Nothing new is stored** apart from the hash in the pipeline state (a JSON checkpoint field, no migration). LLMs produce no number: the range is `financial_math` on stored samples and stored misses.

## Alternatives considered

- **Store the range rows per pipeline (a new table) and render the report from them.** The report would always reproduce, even after the misses are rebuilt. Rejected for now: the first draft's reason ("misses change only when the scoring code changes") was wrong; they also change when the table is rebuilt after new actuals are filed (analyst). But the rule is then M9's own: a range build fails reproduce when its misses change, and `reproduce` names the cause and the command that rewrites the report. If rebuilds become routine (M10's batches, #162), a stored copy can come with them.
- **Put the range inside section 2 (Paths).** Closer to the numbers it qualifies, but section 2 runs per variant to year 5 while the range stops at quarter 4, and inserting it would renumber old reports. A section after hindsight keeps old reports byte-identical.
- **Re-run the track record per shocked variant.** It is the baseline's rolling test either way; computing it again triples the time for the same numbers.
- **Key the range seed on the pipeline's case id, or on (run, seed, n, shocks) instead of `sim_id`.** A run can have several simulations, and `sim_id` names exactly one set of samples. The analyst noted a from-scratch rebuild could number simulations differently; stored simulations are never renumbered, and a rebuild from scratch re-runs the pipelines and their reports with them.

## Product impact

Serves brief Q5 and principle 4 on screens that show a pipeline run. F2's first screen shows a baseline run (`docs/designs/f2-web-app.md`) and was never blocked by this; its baseline range is v0.9.8's.

- New: `run_ranges(conn, run_id, sim_id=S)` and `ranges run --sim-id S`. `to_dict` gains `sim_id` (None on a baseline call); each quarter gains `revenue_center` and `ebit_center` (money, in the run's currency like the bands; "millions" in the report is formatting only); each band gains `revenue_vs_center` and `ebit_vs_center` (fractions, as defined in item 2). `basis` can be `"shocked"`; `n_samples` is the simulation's.
- **What the screen shows as the forecast:** `*_center`, on every basis, so the band is always around the number shown. `*_forecast` is the forecast without shocks ("sin choques") on a shocked range; on a baseline range the two are equal. Read the change as `*_vs_center`.
- New codes: `shocked_plus_misses` (`sim_id`), `coverage_is_baseline` (`basis`), `coverage_skipped`, `errors_stale` (`as_of`, `errors_filed_to`). Nothing renamed or removed; no field changes meaning.
- Which simulation: a pipeline's state (`graph.final_state(conn, folders, pipeline_id)`) holds `sim[variant].sim_id`, masked being M8's headline variant; a run's pipelines are in `pipeline_runs` by `run_id`. A call that lists a run's simulations with their variants is backlog (filed at merge). A simulation made outside a pipeline (`python -m domains.analysis simulate`) works the same way; it has no variant.
- Timing: a shocked call with `coverage=False` costs the baseline call's band time plus under 0.01 s; with coverage, about the baseline call's 12 seconds. The answer is deterministic for a `sim_id` and an `errors_sha256`, so the app may keep it keyed by both.

## Results on real data (Step 3, nov.05.2026)

On the snapshot restored from `warren-20261104T202442Z` (schema 21). 48 finished pipelines have simulations (77 simulations, all 10,000 samples); the other 121 had no shock and get the baseline table only.

- **Three pipelines re-rendered** with the section (not written to disk: these pipelines' reports are not in the snapshot): CAT_2026-08-15_p1 (named and masked, two simulations), WMT_2025-08-21_p1 (named only), AAPL_2024-08-15_p1 (masked has a simulation, named has no shock). The whole section takes 2.5–3.8 seconds; the track record is nearly all of it.
- **The shocks move the band, they barely widen it.** Over the 63 simulations with a pooled quarter-4 band, the median revenue width ((p90 − p10) ÷ centre) is 37.1% for the baseline band and 37.6% for the shocked one; the centre moves by 0.5% of revenue at the median and up to 10.0% (CAT_2026-08-15: +9.9% in every quarter, both variants). On CAT the shocked revenue band is 1–4 points wider per quarter (32.9% → 35.7% at quarter 1); on WMT and AAPL it is within ±2.5 points either way, which is the draw-to-draw noise of a different seed, not the shocks.
- **A finding for the backlog, not this item.** The pooled EBIT band applies every company's margin misses to Walmart's thin margin, so WMT's quarter-1 EBIT band runs from −2,413 to 29,980 (USD millions) around an 8,104 forecast; its own track record says so (100% of 197 past forecasts inside: `too_wide`). This is M9's pooled method as built; scaling misses by each company's own volatility is #125.
