# Backlog #144: the error range and its track record on any run

Issue: https://github.com/sonderbot-ar/warren/issues/144 · Branch `backlog-144-run-ranges` · Version v0.9.8 when merged.

## Problem

The front-end team's request R3 (brief question 5, `docs/product/brief.md` §5; design principle 4: every forecast shows the uncertainty the engine measures). M9 (v0.9) gives each forecast quarter a p10–p90 range from the baseline's own past misses, but only for the 728 stored monthly origins and the backtest cases (`domains/evaluation/ranges.py`, `build_rows`). A run made today, or any run F2's first screen shows, has no range. And the one coverage figure M9 reports (revenue 83.1% pooled) hides that 4 of 8 companies fell outside 65–95% (v0.9 row of `docs/versions.md`), so a student must see how the range did on this company too.

## What changes

A new call, `domains.evaluation.run_ranges.run_ranges(conn, run_id, n=10_000) -> RunRanges`, and a command, `python -m domains.evaluation ranges run --run-id R [--json]`. For one stored run, as of its own date D:

1. **The range per forecast quarter.** For horizons 1–4, the M9 method unchanged (`ranges.ranges` on `ranges.constant_samples` of the run's own quarterly revenue and EBIT outputs): the misses whose actual was filed on or before D (`ranges.known`), both pools (`pooled`: all companies; `own`: this company), n draws, one generator per pool seeded `ranges.range_seed(f"{run_id}:baseline", pool)`, as M9 seeds its subjects (`ranges.py:subject_rows`, key `<subject id>:<variant>`): for a run the subject id is the run id. Every run gets horizons 1–4 (M9 stopped some origins earlier only because their filings ended; a run's forecast quarters exist regardless). Step 3 calls the same band helper with the origin id as key, on the origin's own stored horizons only, to show equality with M9. Each band carries `p10`, `p50`, `p90`, `n_errors`, `n_quarters`, and the same three as a change from the forecast, computed by `financial_math` so the app never does arithmetic (product-lead; principle 5): revenue `fm.growth(p, forecast)` (the band is forecast × (1 + x)), EBIT `fm.ratio(p − EBIT forecast, revenue forecast)` in margin points of forecast revenue. `method` is `resampled_misses`: M9 draws one past miss per sample and applies it, revenue as forecast × e^miss, EBIT as the forecast margin plus the margin miss on the drawn revenue, so the EBIT band is not the EBIT forecast times its own percentile. p50 is the middle of the draws and can sit off the forecast: that gap is the baseline's typical bias in the misses (professor), and the forecast stays the headline number. A pool with fewer known misses than M9's minimum (`MIN_ERRORS`: 20 pooled, 12 own) has no band and the code `no_history` (params `horizon`, `pool`, `n_errors`, `min_errors`). Quarters 5–8 have no band and the code `beyond_horizon` (params `horizon`, `max_horizon` 4): M9 measured misses for the first four quarters only.
2. **How the range has done so far (coverage).** Per pool, per item, over all companies and over this company: how many past forecast quarters fell inside their p10–p90, below and above. The quarters counted are M9's rolling test, cut at D: baseline ranges of the stored origins dated `ROLLING_FROM` (2021-01-01) or later and **before D**, each built as M9 builds it (from the misses known on the origin's own date, seeded by its origin id), scored only where that quarter's actual (the item's `actual_filed_at`) was filed on or before D. Horizons 1–4 are counted together, as M9's headline does (M9 found coverage flat across horizons, 81–85% for revenue; a note says so).
   - **Rows are not independent quarters** (analyst, professor). Origins are monthly and the baseline only moves when a filing lands, so one target quarter is scored up to 13 times (2,705 revenue rows hold 920 distinct misses on 242 target quarters, analyst's query). Each cell therefore gives `n` (rows), `n_quarters` (distinct company and fiscal quarter) and `n_clusters` (distinct calendar quarters), and its interval is M9's own: the 90% cluster bootstrap by calendar quarter (`ranges_report.cluster_bootstrap`, the interval M9's pass rule uses), not Wilson. A cell with fewer than 12 distinct target quarters (`MIN_QUARTERS`, M9's own-pool minimum) still shows its counts and gets `few_scored` (params `n`, `n_quarters`, `min_quarters`); a run dated on or before 2021-01-01 has none and gets `no_coverage_history` (params `origins_from`, `as_of`).
   - **A verdict the app does not compute** (product-lead, brief Q5). Each cell carries `target` (0.80), `acceptable` ([0.65, 0.95], M9's per-company band) and `verdict`: `held` (share inside the acceptable band), `too_narrow` (below it: the actual fell outside too often) or `too_wide` (above it), None when the cell has a gap; `target_in_interval` (is 80% inside the cluster interval); and on a company cell `differs_from_all` (the all-companies share of the same pool and item lies outside the company's interval). Beside it, `width`: the median p90 − p10 over the p50 (revenue) or over the revenue p50 (EBIT, margin points), M9's `ranges_report.widths`, so a 100% that comes from a very wide range shows as such (professor; Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery 2007).
   - **Changes of reporting basis are counted** (analyst; #162). A quarter whose filing recast the year before (JNJ after Kenvue, KO 2019) is scored as a miss in M9's history, so it widens this company's own band and counts as outside in coverage. Until #162 removes them at the source, the call runs the v0.9.7 detector (`hindsight.against_actuals` with the filings public by D, `restated_basis`) on this company's origins and reports, per own-pool band, `basis_changes` (how many of its known misses are basis changes) and, per company cell, `basis_changes` and `basis_changes_outside`. Other companies are not checked (about 7 seconds per company on the restored data), so pooled bands and all-companies cells carry `basis_unchecked`. Nothing is dropped: the numbers stay M9's.
   - **Which band goes with which figure** (product-lead). The headline band is `pooled` (M9's headline); its track record is the `pooled` cells, `company` for this company and `all` for every company. The `own` band pairs with the `own` cells the same way. The `own`/`all` cell is the own-pool method's calibration across companies, for the method page, not the first screen.
3. **What the range is of.** `basis` is `"baseline"`: the range is the baseline's own error, not the shocks. A note says so (`baseline_only`). The shocked range on a pipeline run (M9's "on top of the simulation") is not built here; see Alternatives.
4. **What it rests on.** `code_differs` is a note when the run's code version is not the one the stored origins were built with (analyst: 177 stored runs come from older versions; their paths matched on the 4 checked, but the misses are the current baseline's). Also: `errors_sha256` (`ranges.pairs_sha256` of the misses known on D, so a caller can tell if the stored history behind a range has changed since), `errors_filed_to` (the newest filing date among them) and `currency` (`montecarlo._currency`, the revenue concepts' unit; Decision 11, explicit currency).

All values are raw and unrounded, in the run's currency; EBIT's band is in money (M9 applies the margin error on the drawn revenue, `fm.apply_margin_error`). Gaps are coded (`reported.Coded`: `code`, `params`, `text` in English), never raised, so the app always gets an answer. A run that is not stored raises, as `against_actuals` does.

**Point in time.** Everything comes from data public by D: the misses (filed by D), the origins scored for coverage (dated before D, their actuals filed by D, their ranges from misses filed by their own date). Nothing is written. The run's own forecast is the stored run's output, which reads only data public by D apart from the owner's method choices (`user_assumptions.csv`), which are fixed by design, as for every M9 origin (M9 design, Definitions). Tests in Step 1 check the D boundary for both the bands and the coverage.

**Same numbers as M9.** For a stored origin's own run, the call's bands with the origin's id as the seed key equal the stored M9 build's rows exactly, and its coverage cut at the build's date equals the M9 report's rolling coverage on the same rows (Step 3, real data). Computing the 718 origin ranges takes about 5 seconds and the basis check on one company about 7 (measured on the restored data, nov.03.2026), so a call takes about 12 seconds. The answer is deterministic for a run and an `errors_sha256`, so the app may keep it keyed by both.

## Steps

- **Step 0.** This design doc; no build plan change (a backlog item).
- **Step 1.** `domains/evaluation/run_ranges.py` with `run_ranges`, `RunRanges.to_dict`, the helpers `bands`, `scored_origin_rows`, `basis_flags` and `coverage_cells`. Tests (`tests/evaluation/test_run_ranges.py`, synthetic `baseline_errors` and `forecast_origins` rows in a migrated test database plus a stored run, numbers known by construction): the bands equal `ranges.ranges` called directly with the same seed; a miss filed on D is used and one filed the day after is not; quarters 5–8 are `beyond_horizon`; 19 pooled misses give `no_history` and 20 give a band; the own pool counts only the company's misses; coverage counts an origin dated D−1 but not one dated D, and a quarter filed after D is not counted; inside/below/above sum to n; one target quarter scored by three origins counts 3 rows, 1 quarter; a cell with 11 distinct quarters is `few_scored` and 12 is not; the interval is `cluster_bootstrap`'s on the same rows; the verdict is `held` at 0.65 and 0.95, `too_narrow` at 0.64, `too_wide` at 0.96; `differs_from_all` when the all-companies share is outside the company's interval; a flagged basis change is counted in its own band and cell and `basis_unchecked` sits on pooled bands and all-companies cells; the band's change from the forecast equals `fm.growth` and `fm.ratio` on its own numbers; `code_differs` on a run with another code version; a run before 2021 is `no_coverage_history`; the same call twice gives the same dict; `to_dict` round-trips through `json`; an unknown run raises. Exit: green.
- **Step 2.** The command `ranges run --run-id R [--json]`: a table per pool of p10 / p50 / p90 per quarter in millions, the coverage lines, the notes; `--json` prints `to_dict` only. Tests (`tests/evaluation/test_cli.py`): text output names the basis, the pools and the coverage; `--json` is plain JSON with the fields below; an unknown run exits 1 with a message. Exit: green; ruff clean.
- **Step 3.** Real data, on the restored snapshot: on every stored origin run, the bands with the origin id as seed key equal the `ranges_2026-11-02_1` rows (all 11,416), and `errors_sha256` cut at 2026-11-02 equals that build's; coverage cut at 2026-11-02 equals the M9 rolling coverage from the stored rows (pooled and per company); one run per company as of 2026-11-02 (the latest ended day the data holds) gets bands and coverage, written below with the timing. The equality covers every status (`ok`, `no_history`, `missing`). The per-company runs are stored with `python -m domains.analysis dcf --ticker T --as-of 2026-11-02`. `python -m domains.evaluation ranges reproduce` still passes. Exit: equalities hold.

**Exit criterion:** all tests pass; Step 3's equalities hold; `docs/team-sync.md` names the call and its fields.

## Alternatives considered

- **Read the ranges from a stored M9 build.** Rejected: a build covers only origins and cases, and its coverage is cut at the build's date, not the run's. Computing on the call gives the same numbers (Step 3) and works for any run and date.
- **Put the range in the pipeline report now.** Deferred: a report is compared again by `reproduce`, and the range moves when the stored misses are scored again (new actuals filed). It needs the misses' hash in the pipeline state first. Filed as a backlog item; the app gets the range from the call.
- **The shocked range on pipeline runs.** Deferred to the same item: it needs the pipeline's simulation samples again (`montecarlo.sample_lines` with the stored seed and shocks), and the front-end's first screen asks for the baseline's range first (brief Q5).
- **Coverage per horizon.** Not now: at h4 a company has about 20 scored quarters since 2021, too few for a share to mean much. The pooled-horizon figure is M9's headline; per horizon can come with #125 (scaling by each company's volatility).
- **Store the range with each run.** Rejected: it would add a table whose rows go stale when misses are rescored; the call is deterministic and cheap.

## Product impact

Serves brief question 5 (the forecast with its range) and design principle 4. Q5's "Hoy" line changes from "En parte … not for a new run. Request R3 (#144)" to say the range and the company's coverage exist for any run, the baseline's only (the shocked range still waits). No existing output, name or field changes.

Adds `domains.evaluation.run_ranges.run_ranges(conn, run_id)` and `to_dict`, and the command `ranges run`. Fields of `to_dict`:

- top: `run_id`, `ticker`, `as_of`, `currency`, `basis` (`"baseline"`), `n_samples`, `errors_sha256`, `errors_filed_to`, `quarters[]`, `coverage[]`, `notes[]` (codeds);
- a quarter: `period` (the run's label, e.g. `FY2026Q3`), `horizon` (1–8), `revenue_forecast`, `ebit_forecast`, `bands[]`;
- a band: `pool` (`pooled` or `own`), `method` (`resampled_misses`), `n_errors`, `n_quarters`, `revenue` and `ebit` (each `{p10, p50, p90}` in money, or None), `revenue_vs_forecast` (each `{p10, p50, p90}` as a fraction: the band is forecast × (1 + x)) and `ebit_vs_forecast` (fractions of forecast revenue: margin points), `basis_changes` (own pool; None where unchecked), `missing` (a coded or None), `notes[]`;
- a coverage cell: `pool`, `scope` (`all` or `company`), `item`, `inside`, `below`, `above`, `n` (rows), `n_quarters`, `n_clusters`, `share` (a fraction, 0 to 1), `interval` (`[low, high]`, 90% cluster bootstrap by calendar quarter, or None), `target` (0.80), `acceptable` ([0.65, 0.95]), `verdict` (`held`, `too_narrow`, `too_wide` or None), `target_in_interval`, `differs_from_all` (company cells; None on all-companies cells), `width` (median (p90 − p10) / p50; EBIT over the revenue p50), `basis_changes` and `basis_changes_outside` (company cells; None where unchecked), `origins_from` (2021-01-01), `missing` (a coded or None), `notes[]`;
- money is in whole currency units (divide by a million for a "millions" header: formatting only);
- codes: `no_history`, `beyond_horizon`, `few_scored`, `no_coverage_history`, `basis_unchecked`, `horizons_pooled`, and the run-level notes `baseline_only`, `code_differs`.

How the app gets a `run_id`: `python -m domains.analysis dcf --ticker T [--as-of D]` (or the library call behind it) stores the run and prints its id; a pipeline run's id is in its report header. Exxon's filings stop in August 2026 (#126): its runs say so already through the run notes, not here.
